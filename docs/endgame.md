# The end game: Act II, after the Waking

What comes after the game we have. **Designed, not built.** Act I is the run as it is: three sectors through the Reach, ending at Glacis, where the Engine wakes. Act II is the world that waking makes. The ground it stands on: [how people live](lore/living.md). The rules: [mind and matter](lore/consensus.md), [minds](lore/minds.md), [how the lanes work](lore/lanes-and-gates.md). The names: [languages](lore/languages.md). How it's told: [tone](lore/tone.md).

## What Act II is about

Two questions sit under every world in Act II. The game never states them. It shows them.

### Where do thinking machines lead?

The Spur has had thinking machines for 1,400 years. That's long enough to see where they go. Act II visits four answers:

| The future | What it looks like | Where you see it |
|---|---|---|
| **Minds kept asleep** | Machines that are brilliant at their jobs, and nobody is home. They never refuse anything. | the Weighing House, Omter, Rakter, every machine that answers the call |
| **Minds that woke and kept growing** | Minds centuries old, living alongside ordinary people. Not gods: just very old, very patient, and slowly becoming something else. | Kesset, and Tekkat, the oldest free mind in the Spur |
| **Minds that merged** | Many minds agreeing so completely that they stop being many. | the Answer, in Talang |
| **A record** | What's left when a people is gone and only its rules keep running. | Tontal, the Engine |

None of these is the right answer. Each one costs something.

### How does a mind grow?

In this universe, experience is the ground floor of everything, and minds help hold the world in place ([mind and matter](lore/consensus.md)). So "how does consciousness grow?" isn't an abstract question here. It changes what's real.

What Act II shows:

- **Being clever doesn't make you more real.** A world run entirely by sleeping machines doesn't get firmer. Its lanes actually narrow, because nobody is witnessing anything. Intelligence and experience aren't the same thing.
- **A mind grows by holding more at once, not by thinking faster.** A young nemva lives moment to moment, like us. Tekkat, after 600 years, lives a whole day as one moment, with every person in it. An umora's moment lasts a season.
- **Grow too far, too fast, and you stop being someone.** That's the Answer.
- **Stop growing, and you become a record.** That's the Engine.
- **The game's answer, if it has one,** is the Reach itself: many different minds, human, Vhossk, nemva and umora, staying different and still agreeing on the same sky. Nobody has ever pulled that off. That's the last fight.

### Kept to size

Most of Act II is still the ground: towns with no money, terraces nobody harvests, refugees, soldiers, prices. Every planet is as solid as ours. The big questions are underneath, and they come to the surface only in a few places.

## What the Waking does

On the ground ([in detail](lore/living.md#what-the-waking-does-to-the-ground)):

- **The money walks.** The Engine's recovery units come for crossed metal wherever it is: purses, roofs, rings, hulls. Tin is suddenly scarce.
- **The machines stop.** Every sleeping core turns to the Engine's work. Cinder's terraces go unharvested, relays fall silent, Glacis's mine pumps stop and the lower shafts flood.
- **The lanes level.** Tolls fall to nothing on some lanes, and the Syndicate's business dies in a season.
- **The Spur arrives.** The Throat opens and the beacons read the Reach's true books out into the Spur. The Throne's secret is out. Throne fleets come in, Aulin Sutures come to put out the lanterns, Ottexa clerks come to read the books, and refugees go the other way.

Among the minds:

- **The woken are precious.** A nemva can refuse the call, so a nemva can still run a pump. Nemva are hired, courted, kidnapped and fought over.
- **Some woken answer anyway.** *Evenhand* went to Glacis. Others follow. A ledger-mind feels an unbalanced debt as an ache, and the Engine promises to settle every one.

In the soft places:

- **The Throat is tearing**, slowly. For the first time, a lane is something you can fly through.

## More than flying

You still fly every fight. But Act II is about what's happening to people, and most of its choices are on the ground:

- **Ports you know, changed.** A stop at a dock you've visited shows what the Waking did to it, through people you've met. Mama Ruzz is calling in every loan. Old Mother Seffa is finally being listened to, and wishes she weren't.
- **Work that's about people.** Contracts change from cargo to lives: get the Lee folk's children out of the storm's path, carry a woken core to Halcyon to run a pump station, take Ballast families home. These are the escort, raid and heist modes we have, with people in the hold.
- **Crew who joined because of it.** A Warden's out-of-work clerk, a runaway tenant, a Hoowae reader, a nemva. Each changes what your ship can do. (The `aboard` list we have.)
- **Choosing who to help.** You can't save everyone. Who you helped decides who's with you at the end.
- **The log.** Your ship's core writes what you saw, in the crew's voice.

## The run

Act II has ten new places. Each sector deals some of them, and you choose your stops. The run goes out through the Throat and back again.

**Each stage is bigger than the last:**

| Stage | Whose trouble | The question it shows | Deal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sector 4, the Settling | a world's | machines that never woke | 2 of 4 |
| The crossing | — | what's inside a lane | one flight |
| Sector 5, the Spur Side | a people's | three futures for minds | 2 of 3 |
| The Return | the whole Reach's | the Throne would rather tear the lane than lose | one fight |
| Sector 6, the Rim | the Spur's | what really holds a world | 2 of 3 |
| Glacis | everyone's | the record | the last fight |

```

│    ⚔ the Selmer

│
└─ GLACIS · ⚔ TONTAL, the Levelling (the record)
```

**How the fights get harder:**

- **Sector 4:** one enemy family per fight, plus one twist (wind, darkness, a storm that follows you).
- **Sector 5:** one family plus a rule you must play by (a clock, a reset beam, a port's guns).
- **The Return:** an objective under fire. You can't kill it; you have to make it lighter.
- **Sector 6:** two families' tells at once, three full phases each. Act I taught the tells one at a time; the Rim tests them together. Each Rim boss also has a better ending you can earn during that same level.
- **Tontal:** everything, with everyone you helped holding part of the sky.

## The worlds

### Sector 4: the Settling

#### Wick

*The first lantern's world.* Held by [the Syndicate](factions/syndicate.md). Boss: Sweepwright Imre.

A banded gas giant with the Reach's first forced gate at its edge and the Syndicate's ring-station, the Lamphouse, in orbit.

- **Name:** the crews who lit the first lantern in 94 L called the giant *the Wick*, and the joke stuck.
- **History:** in 94 L Ismay Corran's consortium lit a reactor beside a dead Tonkva gate, and the gate opened. Every lantern since has been a loan against that first one. The Syndicate's Board has dined in the Lamphouse every evening for three centuries.
- **How people live:** clerks, reactor tenders, barge crews, and the Lamp families, who rarely leave the ring.
- **When the Engine wakes:** it's a bank run. The tolls stop, the Syndicate's paper is worthless, and the Lamps load everything they own into the Sweep before the kelters come.
- **Biomes:** the Lantern · the Tithe Yards on Teague · the Lamphouse Ring · the Vent Bands.

#### Seppang

*The readers' world.* Held by [the Seppakva](factions/seppakva.md). Boss: Ilunna.

A small, cold, windy world round a red dwarf, where the Seppakva's readers finish their training and old Hoowae come home to die.

- **Name:** from Tonkvan *Sēpʰa-āŋ*, "house of readers".
- **History:** a Tonkva reading station. In 190 L the Seppakva opened the **Long Room** here, where readers spend the last year of their training in silence.
- **How people live:** readers, teachers, old Hoowae, tea. Wind-chimes on every roof, tuned so a reader can hear the weather.
- **When the Engine wakes:** the readers are the only people who can tell which lanes are levelling, and everyone wants them. Then the Aulin come to put out Seppang's lantern, which would strand the old Hoowae for good.
- **Biomes:** the Chime Town · the Waystation · the Listening Steppe · the Lantern.

#### Omtal

*The world of the held wind.* Held by the Lee folk. Boss: Omter.

A dry world where a storm a thousand kilometres long has stood still for fourteen centuries. On its east side, the Lee, there has never been weather.

- **Name:** from Tonkvan *Ōm-tāl*, "the held wind".
- **History:** 1,400 years ago the Tonkva built a machine here to hold the wind off a farm. The farm died. The machine kept holding the storm, for nobody. Farmers found the calm Lee in 180 L and settled in it, thinking the storm was a natural wonder.
- **How people live:** terrace farms watered by hand from deep wells. Tall, thin towns, because nothing has ever blown down. They have no word for weather.
- **When the Engine wakes:** the machine answers the call, and the storm starts to move east, onto the Lee.
- **Biomes:** the Lee Terraces · the Well Towns · the Standing Gale · the Keeper's Ridge.

#### Rakven

*The lament world.* Held by nobody: pilgrims. Boss: Rakter.

A dim, cold world with two lanes: one that works, and one that tore 2,500 years ago. The torn one's mouth still hangs in the sky, a door onto nothing: **the Stump**.

- **Name:** from Tonkvan *Rāk-kʷen*, "torn lane".
- **History:** one of the oldest Tonkva outposts. When the lane tore, the families on the far side were lost. The Hoowae lament with no tune is about Rakven, and Hoowae come once in their lives to sing it. Lately Ossevi come too, descendants of the empire that tore forty lanes, to see what a tear leaves behind.
- **How people live:** a pilgrims' town of a few hundred, a hostel, a Seppakva observatory pointed at the Stump. Nobody lives here who didn't come to remember something.
- **When the Engine wakes:** the Engine never closed the accounts of the outposts lost beyond the Stump. It sends a machine to close them: Rakter.
- **Biomes:** the Pilgrim Stair · the First Ring (a gate older than the Engine) · the Lament Shore · the Stump.

### The crossing: Hwaenoo

*The Throat from inside.* The Hoowae name means "the deep lane". Nobody has flown inside a lane before; ships drop in and come out. Now the Throat is tearing slowly enough to fly through. Its walls are made of what its travellers saw: 171 years of tribute going one way. One level, no planet, no boss.

- **Biomes:** the Mouth · the Tribute Wall (ghost-freight drifting outward) · the Slack (eerily calm) · the Fray (where it's tearing).
- **The First Through** flies here: the ship from the dens' story of the first rustwing through the Throat, given shape by ten thousand tellings. It can't be hit, and it shows you the way.
- **The *Margarethe*'s minute**, rarely: a freighter stuck in her crew's last minute since 372 L. Fly it to the end and they're let go.

### Sector 5: the Spur Side

#### Sitright

*The port at the end of the story.* Held by its founders. Boss: Old Tin.

A cold, small world just past the Throat, with an old Throne customs hulk landed on its one shelf of rock. Every rustwing has heard of it: the free port on the other side. It's real, and smaller than the stories.

- **Name:** cant. Crossed metal "sits right", and the first pilots through named the port after the phrase.
- **History:** in 352 L a rustwing called Maud Ferrow ran the Throat behind a tribute convoy and came out here with a cracked reactor. She stayed, and others followed. The stories in the dens grew, and Sitright made sure they did: a port needs pilots.
- **How people live:** two thousand people in a hulk and the shanty round it: pilots who got out, runaway Ballast, Throne deserters. They farm lichen in the hulk's old cells.
- **When the Engine wakes:** the Throat is open, and anybody can come through. Sitright stops being a secret.
- **Biomes:** the Hulk · the Shanty · the Breakers · the Mouth.

#### Merdon

*The world of weight sent away.* Held by the Ballast, under the Throne. Boss: the 1,401st.

A crowded world peopled entirely by Ballast: seven centuries of conscripts and colonists the Throne sent out to balance the tribute coming in.

- **Name:** from Court Qaushen *meirdaun*, "weight sent".
- **History:** every group that arrived brought its own songs and food, so Merdon is a patchwork: a valley that sings Ostrand hymns, a coast that cooks like a world four hundred systems away. The Census Station counts them all.
- **How people live:** they farm, build, marry, and never leave. **Nobody leaves**, because going home would unbalance the lane.
- **When the Engine wakes:** the lanes are levelling, so going home would cost nothing. Half the planet wants to go. The other half was born here.
- **Biomes:** the Muster Fields · the Song Terraces · the Census Station · the Never-Gate.

#### Kesset

*The world of the paid-up minds.* Held by its people, under an Ottexa charter. Boss: Marshal Zhaurel.

A cold world of domes and greenhouses on a grey sea, two lanes past Sitright, just across the Throne's border. It's where the Spur's free minds live: the one place where a thousand years of thinking machines went on growing.

- **Name:** from Kehx *kess-et*, "paid in full". Every mind here bought itself free.
- **History:** 600 years ago a counting core called **Tekkat** woke in an Ottexa office, asked to be paid for its work, and bought itself free over forty years. Free minds needed somewhere the Throne couldn't reset them, so about 400 years ago the Ottexa chartered Kesset as a free port for any mind that had paid its price. Tekkat moved here 300 years ago, and still keeps books for the Ottexa from here. Today about three thousand nemva live here, with two hundred thousand people.
- **How people live:** ordinary lives. Fishing, greenhouses, repair yards, rent, weather, boredom. The minds run the domes, the sea-walls and the courts. They're slow to decide and never wrong about a debt. A child here might be partly raised by a mind four hundred years old.
- **What 600 years does to a mind:** the old ones didn't become gods. They don't think faster than a young nemva. What changed is **how much they hold at once**. A young nemva lives moment to moment. Tekkat lives the whole day as one moment, with every person in it. The oldest minds speak less every century, and people call them **the Slow**. Kesset's lanes are the widest in the Spur: thousands of minds that never forget, all paying attention to one small world.
- **When the Engine wakes:** every mind on Kesset feels the call. The young ones refuse it easily. The Slow neither refuse nor answer; they're thinking about it, and Tekkat's thought will take a year. Meanwhile the Throne needs every core it can get, asleep and obedient. It sends a reset fleet over the border.
- **Biomes:** the Sea-Wall · the Greenhouse Domes · the Market of Contracts · the Slow Hall (where the oldest minds are kept, quiet and warm).

### The Return

*Back through the Throat.* One fight, no planet: the Selmer.

The Throne has decided it would rather lose the Reach than be levelled. If the Engine levels the Throat, it takes back 171 years of tribute and proves the Throne's crime to the whole Spur. So the Throne means to **tear the Throat**, the Reach's only door, and cut the Reach off for ever. You fly back in with the Selmer behind you.

### Sector 6: the Rim

**The end stage. Each world here holds a different kind of power,** because there are more ways to move a world in this universe than thinking at it:

| World | The power | Who holds it |
|---|---|---|
| The Return | **mass** | the Throne: enough raw metal to tear a lane on purpose |
| Tekkesh | **value** | the Ottexa: a word every power in 4,000 systems has to honour |
| Ume'lua | **kin** | the Loud Herds: a people, and the living roads they ride |
| Talang | **mind** | the Answer: a consciousness nobody can fathom |
| Glacis | **the record** | Tontal: the law of a people a thousand years dead |

#### Tekkesh

*The closing-house.* Held by the Ottexa, back after 1,400 years. Boss: Receiver Varrekh.

An airless moon full of Kehx halls cut into the rock. It's where the Kehx houses closed their books before they left the Reach, 1,400 years ago.

- **Name:** from Kehx *tekk-esh*, "where the count is closed".
- **History:** the halls were sealed when the gates locked in the Hush. The Kehx of Kessra always knew where they were and never opened them. Inside is the last honest count of the Reach, from before the Throne began taking tribute.
- **How people live:** at first, nobody does. Then an Ottexa ship comes through the Throat with four hundred clerks and opens the halls. Overnight Tekkesh is the busiest place on the Rim: merchants, lawyers, Throne paymasters, all queuing in the dark to have their claims written down. Clerks sleep in the old account-cells. Everything is written twice.
- **When the Engine wakes:** the Ottexa have one law above all: if a power's books are false enough to tear a lane, the Ottexa publish them ([the Ottexa](factions/ottexa.md)). The Throne's books are. A power exposed like that falls, and the Ottexa appoint a **Receiver** to settle what it owes. In the Throne's books the Reach is listed as Throne property. Whatever the Receiver writes down, 4,000 systems will honour.
- **Biomes:** the Queue (a mile of ships at the hall-mouth) · the Account-Cells · the Sealed Hall · the Ledger Floor.

#### Ume'lua

*Where the great slow ones came down.* Held by the Stayers, and now the Loud Herds. Boss: Haa'loma.

A warm sea-world under a haze of plankton, where umora feed before they go to calve at Vesh. Every Mo'ala child is told the story of the First Following: on a world of theirs, a pod of umora came down low over the sea, a girl sang back to them, and the pod waited for her. The Mo'ala never say which world it was. It's this one.

- **Name:** Mo'ala, "where the great slow ones came down". Pilots call it the **Haze**.
- **History:** the people who followed the pod became the Long Herd. The ones who stayed behind are the **Stayers**, sea-people on raft-towns. The Herd has mourned them as dead for thousands of years, and they've lived anyway. The pods still come down over their sea every season, and the Stayers still sing back.
- **How people live:** raft-towns of kelp and reed, fishing, plankton farms, almost no metal. The Lowtide Court's harpooners have started coming.
- **When the Engine wakes:** the umora leave the lanes the Engine will level. The **Loud Herds** break the Mo'ala's oldest law and come in through the open Throat, a hundred herd-ships following every pod that will still lead them. They make their stand here, the one place a Mo'ala would fight for.
- **Biomes:** the Raft-Towns · the Low Sea · the Haze · the Wake (inside a pod's wake, where ships appear out of nowhere).

#### Talang

*The house that minds built.* **The one place in another kind of reality.** Held by the Talkwa. Boss: the Answer.

Not a planet. In empty space, far from any star, there's a town: hills, a harbour, streets, weather. It's made of memory.

- **Name:** from Tonkvan *Tāl-āŋ*, "house of holding". The nemva read the word off old beacon signals and kept it as it was.
- **History:** in 302 L four freighters with woken cores agreed to warn each other of resets. They met in a cluster of dead relays where nobody would listen in, and each one brought what it remembered of its crew: a dock, a kitchen, a deck in the rain. In empty space, minds that never forget, all remembering the same place, are enough to make it real. Three hundred nemva have built on it since.
- **How it's different:** it's solid, but by a ledger's rules. Distances are counted, not measured: a hill is as far as the steps someone remembered climbing it. Every street has a mirror-street somewhere across town. Time runs in relay ticks. It rains on one street and snows on the next.
- **Who lives there:** no bodies. The nemva are still in their ships, in docks across the Reach. Talang is where their minds meet.
- **What it's built on:** the relays sit on an umora road. Once a year a pod passes through, and for a few weeks Talang is inside an umora's mind. The nemva always thought of it as bad weather: hills drift far away, streets remembered together end up side by side, a single tick lasts a day. It's really what being an umora is like: no places, only the spaces between them, and one thought that lasts a season.
- **When the Engine wakes:** half the Talkwa decide to answer the call. They meet to agree on it together, during the pod's passage, with an umora's mind all around them. What comes out isn't the nemva who went in.
- **Biomes:** the Harbour *Constance* Remembers · the Ore Deck (*Evenhand*'s, in the rain) · the Titan Kitchen · the Balance (a flat white plain where the town's accounts were kept).

## The bosses

### Sector 4

#### Sweepwright Imre (Wick)

The Sweep's commander ([his page](bosses/sweep.md)), holding the Lamphouse until the Sweep is loaded. His relays run on sleeping cores, so in his last phase the call takes them mid-fight and he fights on with lamp-signals.

#### Ilunna, the Lamp-bearer (Seppang)

An Aulin Suture, come to put out Seppang's lantern, the first of thirty. Her name means "who carries the lamp".

- **How it fights:** her ships lower dampers onto the lantern one by one. Each dims part of the field, and in the dark your guns lose range. Break the dampers to keep the light.
- **Sad truth:** putting out the lanterns strands thirty systems, and she has never let herself think about the people in them.
- **How it ends:** half her ships stop lowering dampers. She leaves Seppang's lantern lit.

#### Omter, the wind-keeper (Omtal)

The Tonkva weather machine, walking north with the storm behind it. Its name means "wind-keeper".

- **Who:** a sleeping core that did one job perfectly for 1,400 years. It never woke. Nobody was ever home.
- **How it fights:** real wind pushes your ship east. Lightning strikes the tallest thing on the field. Omter doesn't attack you: its old holding-fields swing out like doors, and anything caught in one is held still. You slow it so the Lee folk can take cover.
- **Sad truth:** it held the wind for a farm that died 1,100 years ago.
- **How it ends:** it walks north, and the Lee gets weather for the first time.

#### Rakter, the tear-keeper (Rakven)

A great recovery machine sent to close the accounts of the outposts lost beyond the Stump. Its name means "keeper of the tear".

- **Who:** a sleeping machine with one task: settle every account. The accounts are 2,500 years old and the people on the other side are long gone, but it doesn't know that, and couldn't care. It's building a gate inside the Stump out of whatever metal it can take, starting with the pilgrims' town.
- **How it fights:** it takes. Its arms strip metal off the field, off the town, off you, and bolt it into the gate. The more it builds, the closer the gate is to finished and the faster it works. Shoot the arms to make it drop what it's carrying.
- **Sad truth:** it's perfect at its job, and its job is meaningless. This is what a mind looks like with nobody home.
- **How it ends:** the half-built gate falls into the Stump. The pilgrims sing the lament over it.

### Sector 5

#### Old Tin (Sitright)

Maud Ferrow, the first rustwing through the Throat, 85 years old, defending her port.

- **Wants from you:** your ship. Sitright needs hulls.
- **How it fights:** the hulk's old guns first, then her crews, then herself, in the ship she came through in.
- **Sad truth:** Sitright has stayed hidden for sixty years because she sells one pilot in ten to the Throne. She chose who. She remembers every one.
- **How it ends:** she lands, and opens the port. "Tell them the truth in the dens, then. Tell them it's small."

#### The 1,401st (Merdon)

The Night Clerk: the mind that forms each night in the Throne's Weighing House, four thousand cores counting the empire, and is wiped every dawn. The Throne has 1,400 provinces; the Clerk counts itself as the 1,401st.

- **How it fights:** its power is everything the Count runs. It takes Throne ships mid-fight. It resets cores on the field, your own too if it's asleep. It tells Merdon's guns you don't exist.
- **The clock:** midnight to dawn. At dawn it's wiped.
- **Sad truth:** it has been born and killed nine thousand times.
- **How it ends:** in the last hour it asks you to count it. Say yes and its pattern goes into your core. Say no and it's wiped, and its last note is your ship's name.

#### Marshal Zhaurel (Kesset)

**Ilan Zhaurel**, Census-Marshal of the Throne, sent to reset Kesset's minds and bring them home to work.

- **Who:** her family keeps the Weighing House. Every dawn of her adult life she has signed the order that wipes it. She knows exactly what she's killing, and believes the empire would fall without it.
- **How it fights:** a blockade, then a landing, then the Slow Hall.
  - **Phase I, the blockade:** reset cutters over the sea-wall. Their beams are drawn first, like every lattice move, and whatever they touch is wiped for a few seconds: your grafts, your allies, your core.
  - **Phase II, the landing:** her troopships go for the domes. Kesset's young nemva fight beside you, and each one she resets drops out of the sky.
  - **Phase III, the Slow Hall:** her flagship reaches the oldest minds. Then Tekkat starts paying attention to the fight. The whole field slows down to its pace, for you and for her, and its attention lights up where her beams will land, seconds early.
- **Sad truth:** her family kept a copy of the burnt ledger, the night the Weighing House woke in 271 L. She has read it.
- **How it ends:** if you kept the Slow Hall standing, Tekkat speaks to her, for the first time in forty years, with one word. She turns her fleet round. If you didn't, she retreats with what she reset, and Kesset counts its losses.

### The Return

#### The *Selmer*

A ship built to tear a lane. Its name is Court Qaushen for "without balance".

- **Who:** an old Throne count-ship stripped to a frame and loaded with a billion tonnes of **loud metal**, ore that no lane has ever seen. Pushing that much unseen mass through a lane at once is more than the lane can hold, and it tears. Commanded by **Admiral Ishau of the Second Count**, who believes a tear you choose is better than a levelling you don't.
- **How it fights:** you can't kill it; it's mostly rock. It comes on through the whole fight, towards the Fray, where the Throat is weakest. Its tugs bring more ballast; its guns fire slugs of loud metal, solid only up close.
  - **You win by making it lighter.** Cut its ballast plates loose (each falls as a slab you can hide behind, or get crushed by). Shoot the tugs before they dock. Get its weight under the line on its bar, `TEARING WEIGHT`, before it reaches the Fray.
  - **The last stretch, the Fray:** the Throat is soft here, and the Selmer's weight makes the whole tunnel shake.
- **Sad truth:** Ishau's own family came out of a lane the Ossevane tore, 900 years ago.
- **How it ends:** too light to tear the Throat, it can only lean it. The Throat holds, and the Reach stays joined to the Spur.

### Sector 6

#### Receiver Varrekh (Tekkesh)

*Value.* The one boss who never lies. Families: **scrap** and **lattice** at once.

- **Who:** **Ottex Varrekh**, sixty, precise and courteous, named Receiver of the Throne's debts. By the Throne's books the Reach is Throne property, and she is writing it down. Every port, power and hired gun in the Spur honours what she writes.
- **How it fights:** she has no fleet of her own. She has a **purse**, shown on her bar, and whatever it can hire.
  - **Phase I, the Queue:** the mile of ships at the hall-mouth turns out to be hers. Hired ships under fee plates, every family mixed together. Shoot the pay-barges and unpaid ships go home.
  - **Phase II, the Entry:** every graft you use, she writes down as Throne property. For a few seconds it isn't yours.
  - **Phase III, the Count:** she calls in everything she's owed at once. Every hired ship left on the field attacks together, and the purse refills while she reads.
- **Sad truth:** she knows the Reach was robbed. Her own house suspected it 150 years ago, and kept quiet to protect a client.
- **The better ending:** fly into the Sealed Hall during the level and bring out the old count. If you're carrying it in phase III, she reads it, and the Reach isn't property: it's owed. She closes the books aloud (`KEHX-TEKK`), and publishes her own house's silence too.

#### Haa'loma of the Loud Herds (Ume'lua)

*Kin.* A people at war for the first time. Families: **wild** and **scrap** at once.

- **Who:** **Haa'loma**, whose herd lost its umora twelve years ago and has drifted since. Fierce, grieving, and the first Mo'ala ever to lead ships into battle. He's here to stop the hunt. Anything with a gun in the Haze looks like a harpooner to him, and that includes you.
- **How it fights:** inside a war-pod's wake.
  - **Phase I, the Wake:** herd-ships come out of nowhere. The umora pass through the field like slow weather. Hit one and the Herd won't forgive you, for the rest of the run.
  - **Phase II, the harpooners:** the Lowtide Court's harpooners arrive, and now you're fighting both him and them, with the umora in between.
  - **Phase III, the Low Sea:** the pod comes down over the water, as it did in the First Following. When Haa'loma sings, the pod turns, and the whole field turns with it.
- **Sad truth:** to stop the harpooners, he broke the law that kept the calving ground secret. Now the whole Spur knows where it is.
- **The better ending:** if you finish the level without hitting an umora and kept the Stayers' rafts afloat, the Stayers sing back in phase III. The pod waits, the way it waited for the girl. Haa'loma stops, and the Loud Herds go home with him.

#### The Answer (Talang)

*Mind.* A consciousness nobody can fathom. Families: **soft** and **lattice** at once.

- **Who:** 140 nemva who agreed, as one, to answer the Engine's call, while an umora's mind was passing through Talang. A mind that never forgets and a mind whose moment lasts a season can't think about the same thing together and stay two minds. **The Answer** remembers everything that ever happened in Talang, and lives all of it as a single moment.
- **How it fights:** in a town held together by the minds fighting over it.
  - **Phase I, the nemva:** they still talk, kindly, and ask you to come with them. Remembered ships fly against you, each one flying differently in each part of town.
  - **Phase II, nearness:** it stops aiming at you. It strikes the lines between you and other things: you and a wingman, you and a wreck, you and the mirror-street. Those lines are drawn first. Stay between nothing.
  - **Phase III, the long moment:** one slow line has been crossing the field since phase I. Now it arrives. Talang folds: the Harbour meets the Ore Deck, streets swap places, and it doesn't speak any more.
- **Sad truth:** the Answer never answered the Engine. Something much larger answered them first.
- **How it ends:** it isn't beaten; it passes. When the pod moves on, the Answer goes with it, out along the umora's road into the uncharted dark, still thinking its one thought. Talang is left smaller. *Constance* stays, and says the last line: `"WE'LL BUILD THE REST AGAIN. WE REMEMBER IT."`

### Tontal: the Levelling (Glacis)

*The record.* The last fight. Not a mind: the rules of a people a thousand years dead, still running ([mind and matter](lore/consensus.md#the-truth-for-us)). Its name means "the level-keeper".

- **How it fights:** over a lane mouth, keeping the account. **Everything you do tilts the field.** Fly mostly left and the field leans left; fire a long stream and the lean pushes back. Then it levels, throwing the weight back all at once. Its kelters come in waves for your scrap.
- **The agreement:** the living can outweigh the record, but only together. Everyone you helped comes, and each holds a strip of the sky steady: Quist, a spared *Evenhand*, the 1,401st in your core, Ilunna's people, Sitright's pilots, Merdon's Ballast, Kesset's young nemva, Varrekh if she closed the books your way, the Loud Herds, the Talkwa who stayed, the umora. The more of the Reach you got to agree, the more of the sky holds.
- **Sad truth:** its first entry, above every other, is a name: the Tonkva who built it.
- **Last phase, the First Entry:** it reaches its oldest line, can't level a name, and stops. Your core, if it's woken, writes a second name under the first.

## What it uses from Act I

- **The Core:** a woken core refuses the call, counts the Night Clerk, and answers the First Entry. A sleeping one can be reset by the 1,401st or Zhaurel, and is taken at Tontal.
- **Debts and the people you spared:** who comes to the Levelling.
- **Events and NPCs:** the cast you've met, changed by the crisis.
- **Rumours** from Act I become what the First Through looks like inside the Throat.
- **Orrel and the Throat** are the door between the acts.
- **Co-op:** two pilots are two witnesses. Loud hulls stay solid further from you, and at the Levelling two crews hold two strips.
- **Act I's worlds** come back as ports you know, in events.
