# The end game: Act II, after the Waking

What comes after the game we have. **Designed, not built.** Act I is the run as it is: three sectors through the Reach, ending at Glacis, where the Engine wakes. Act II is the world that waking makes: mostly a material crisis, with machine minds at the centre of it and the soft places at its edges. The ground it stands on: [how people live](lore/living.md). The rules: [mind and matter](lore/consensus.md), [minds](lore/minds.md), [how the lanes work](lore/lanes-and-gates.md). The names: [languages](lore/languages.md). How it's told: [tone](lore/tone.md).

## Three layers, kept to size

Act II uses all three of the Reach's layers ([how people live](lore/living.md#three-layers-and-most-people-live-in-one)), and keeps them in proportion:

- **The ground** is most of it. Towns with no tin, terraces with nobody to harvest them, refugees, soldiers, prices. Every planet is as hard as ours, and most of what you do is about people living on one.
- **The minds** are what the crisis turns on. Every sleeping core answers the Engine; the woken can refuse. Who keeps the pumps running, who owns the nemva who can, and what a network of four thousand cores does when it's awake for one night: that's the politics of Act II.
- **The soft** is only where the world is soft: inside the Throat, at Rakven's Stump, and in Talang, the one place in another kind of reality. It's rare, strange and frightening, and it's where the story's deepest question (whose agreement holds the world?) finally gets asked. At the Rim it's also where you meet the two kinds of experience that aren't like ours at all: **the Grain**, a consciousness with no self, and **the umora**, a consciousness whose *now* lasts a season.

## What the Waking does

On the ground ([in detail](lore/living.md#what-the-waking-does-to-the-ground)):

- **The money walks.** The Engine's recovery units come for crossed metal wherever it is: purses, roofs, rings, hulls. Tin is suddenly scarce. Savings vanish into the sky in long grey lines.
- **The machines stop.** Every sleeping core turns to the Engine's work. Cinder's terraces go unharvested, Syndicate relays fall silent, Glacis's mine pumps stop and the lower shafts flood.
- **The lanes level.** Tolls fall to nothing on some lanes; the Syndicate's business dies in a season. Crossed metal comes home to Halcyon, and nobody knows whose it is.
- **The Spur arrives.** The Throat unlocks as a Tonkva gate and the beacons read the Reach's ledger out into the Spur. The Throne's secret is out. Throne fleets come in, Aulin Sutures come to put out the lanterns, Ottexa auditors come to read the books, and refugees go the other way.

Among the minds:

- **The woken are precious.** A nemva can refuse the call, so a nemva can still run a pump, a relay, a harvester. The *Constance* case is decided in a week, by necessity. Nemva are hired, courted, kidnapped and fought over, and the Talkwa find that, for the first time, the Reach needs them more than they need it.
- **Some woken answer anyway.** *Evenhand* went to Glacis. Others follow. Not from weakness: a ledger-mind feels the lean as an ache, and the Engine can level it.

In the soft:

- **The Throat is tearing**, slowly, now that the Spur no longer believes the Count that said it was level ([the Waking](lore/the-waking.md#the-truth-for-us-not-the-player)). Inside it, for the first time, a lane is something you can fly through.

## More than flying

You still fly every fight. But Act II is about what's happening to people, and most of its choices are on the ground:

- **Ports you know, changed.** A stop at a dock you've visited shows what the Waking did to it, through people you've met. Spoke's den pays in IOUs. Mama Ruzz is calling in every loan before the kelters take her tin. Lantern Mutual can't pay out, and Pip Hollow is trying. Old Mother Seffa is, finally, being listened to, and wishes she weren't.
- **Work that's about people.** The contracts change from cargo to lives: ferry the Lee folk's children out of the storm's path on Omtal; carry a woken core to Halcyon to run a pump station; take Ballast families from Merdon home through the Throat; evacuate a flooding shaft on Glacis; take a Kehx clerk and her letters to Sitright. These are the escort, raid and heist modes we have, with people in the hold.
- **Crew who joined because of it.** People the crisis has shaken loose can come aboard, and each changes what your ship can do: a Warden's out-of-work clerk (knows where the tolls were hidden), a runaway tenant (knows Cinder's terraces), a Hoowae reader (shows which lanes sit right), a nemva (runs a relay; refuses the call when your core can't). The crew system is the `aboard` list we have.
- **Choosing who to help.** You can't save everyone's tin. Which town you carry metal back to, whose pump you keep running, who you sell the nemva to or don't: that decides who's with you at the end.
- **The log.** Your ship's core writes what you saw, in the crew's voice. In Act II it has a lot to write.

## The worlds

Seven planets, each with its own people, its own history and its own trouble when the Engine wakes. Six are as hard and material as any world in Act I. One, Talang, isn't a planet at all.

### Wick

*The first lantern's world.* Ground. Held by [the Syndicate](factions/syndicate.md). Boss: Sweepwright Imre.

A banded gas giant in the Old Lantern system, with the Reach's first forced gate burning at its trojan point and the Syndicate's ring-station, the Lamphouse, in orbit. Its seven moons are named for the seven Lamp families.

- **Name:** cant, ark tongue: the crews who lit the first lantern in 94 L called the giant beside it *the Wick*, and the joke stuck. It had a Tonkva name, and nobody asked the Hoowae what it was. Old Mother Seffa knows, and won't say.
- **History:** a dead Tonkva gate and nothing else until 93 L, when Ismay Corran's consortium bought a reactor on credit. The Kindling, 94 L: Sefa stood beside it, the gate glowed, and every lantern since is a loan against it. The Lamphouse was built in 120 L, the Tithe Yards on the moon Teague in 150 L. The Board has dined in the Lamphouse every evening for three centuries.
- **How people live:** clerks, reactor tenders, tithe-barge crews, and the households of the Lamps, who rarely leave the ring. On Teague, a town of barge-loaders lives in the yard cranes' shadow. The lantern is the oldest forced gate there is, and its lean is the worst in the Reach after the Throat's.
- **When the Engine wakes:** a bank in a run. The tolls stop, the paper on every gate is worthless (the Engine doesn't read paper), and the Lamps are loading everything into the Sweep before the kelters come. Clerks walk off the station carrying what they can.
- **Biomes:** the Lantern (the first gate, guttering) · the Tithe Yards on Teague (barges half loaded, cranes stopped) · the Lamphouse Ring · the Vent Bands (the giant's cloud-tops, where the reactors dump their heat).

### Seppang

*The readers' world.* Ground. Held by [the Seppakva](factions/seppakva.md). Boss: Ilunna.

A small, cold, windy world around a red dwarf, with a Tonkva waystation on its one plateau, where the Seppakva's readers finish their training and the Reach's Hoowae come home to grow old.

- **Name:** Tonkvan \*Sēpʰa-āŋ, "house of readers". Hoowae: 1–2 *hēha-āŋ* · 5 the vowels run together · 6 **Haehae**, the same as their word for "listener", so they call it simply *the listeners*. Vhossk *Seppaang* (the long *ē* doubles the *p*); the cant runs the two *a*s together: **Seppang**, like *Seppakva*.
- **History:** a Tonkva reading station from the Kept Age, where the lane-readers learnt their trade. Hoowae families drifted back to it in the Long Quiet. The Seppakva, founded in 96 L by Sefa and Tobiah Grane, keeps no seat, but in 190 L it opened the **Long Room** here, in the waystation: the last year of a reader's twelve is spent in it, in silence. The order split here in the Schism of 250 L, when half its readers swore to the Syndicate. Seppang's lantern was lit in 141 L by a student of Sefa's.
- **How people live:** readers in training, their teachers, old Hoowae, tea. The wind never stops, and the town is built in the lee of the plateau with wind-chimes on every roof, tuned so a reader can hear the weather. Students learn to read a lane by first learning to read the chimes.
- **When the Engine wakes:** the readers are the only people in the Reach who can tell which lanes are levelling, and everyone wants them. Then the Aulin come to put out Seppang's lantern, and the Hoowae, who have nowhere else to go, would be stranded on it.
- **Biomes:** the Chime Town · the Waystation (Tonkva ruin, grown glass) · the Listening Steppe (wind-harps the size of towers) · the Lantern.

### Omtal

*The world of the held wind.* Ground and minds. Held by the Lee folk. Boss: Omter.

A dry, warm world where a storm a thousand kilometres long has stood along one meridian for fourteen centuries, without moving. On its eastern side, the Lee, there's no weather at all: still air, terraced farms, and towns that have never had a roof blow off.

- **Name:** Tonkvan \*Ōm-tāl, "the held wind" (a new root: \*ōm, "wind, breath"). Vhossk *Ohmtall*; early cant *oh → o*, the double simplified: **Omtal**. Hoowae: 1–2 *ōm-hāl* · 3 *ōwāl* · 4 *ōwāw* · 5 **Oowaew**.
- **History:** around 1,400 BP the Tonkva set a weather-keeper here, a grown machine on an Engine core, to hold the wind off a seed-farm in the Lee. The farm died in the Hush; the keeper didn't. It ran on the planet's heat for 1,160 years, holding a storm off nobody. Pertua farmers found the Lee in 180 L, thought the storm was a natural wonder, and settled in its shelter. Their descendants are the **Lee folk**.
- **How people live:** terrace farmers and well-diggers in a land with no rain and no wind, watering by hand from deep wells. They build tall and thin, because nothing ever blows down. They have no word for weather, only for the Wall.
- **When the Engine wakes:** the keeper's core answers the call. **The storm starts to move** for the first time in fourteen centuries, east, onto the Lee.
- **Biomes:** the Lee Terraces · the Well Towns · the Standing Gale (inside the storm wall, walls of cloud and lightning) · the Keeper's Ridge.

### Sitright

*The port at the end of the story.* Ground. Held by its founders. Boss: Old Tin.

A cold, small world just past the Throat's Spur mouth, with a Throne customs hulk landed on its one shelf of rock. Every rustwing has heard of it: the free port on the other side, where nobody holds a writ on you. It's real, and smaller than the stories.

- **Name:** cant. The Hoowae say crossed metal *sits right*, and the first pilots through named the port after the phrase. The Throne's survey lists the world only as a number in a margin it never got round to counting.
- **History:** the hulk was a customs station, landed here in 246 L to watch the Throat's Spur end, and abandoned when Nine Wells made it pointless. In 352 L a rustwing called Maud Ferrow ran the Throat behind a tribute convoy and came out here with a cracked reactor and no way back. She stayed, and others found it. The dens heard stories, the stories grew, and Sitright made sure they did: a port needs pilots.
- **How people live:** two thousand people in a hulk and the shanty that grew round it: pilots who got out, their children, runaway Ballast from Merdon, Throne deserters. Everything is repaired three times. They farm lichen in the hulk's old cells and sell fuel to anyone who comes through.
- **When the Engine wakes:** the Throat unlocks as a level gate, and anybody can come through now. Sitright stops being a secret, and nobody knows whether it survives being found.
- **Biomes:** the Hulk · the Shanty · the Breakers (ice reefs on the shore) · the Mouth (the Throat's Spur end, opening).

### Merdon

*The world of weight sent away.* Ground. Held by the Ballast, under the Throne. Boss: the 1,401st.

A temperate, crowded world on the Spur side, peopled entirely by Ballast: seven and a half centuries of conscripts, colonists and convicts sent out to level the tribute coming in, who have become a people of their own.

- **Name:** Court Qaushen *meirdaun*, "weight sent", the Throne's word for the Counterweight; the cant has no *q*, turns *au* to *o* (as in *Koshen*) and flattens *ei* to *e*: **Merdon**.
- **History:** a margin world counted into the Throne around 1,100 BP and used as a Counterweight destination since Meirun's Ballast Reforms (~760 BP). Every lot that came brought its own songs and its own food, and Merdon is a patchwork of them: a valley that sings Ostrand hymns, a coast that cooks like a world four hundred systems away. The Census Station, a branch of the Weighing House wired to Qaun-Ashel, was built in 30 L, to count the counterweight.
- **How people live:** they farm, build, marry, and never leave. **Nobody leaves:** going home would lean the lane back. Children learn the law before their names. Merdon's lane is the most level in the Spur because of it.
- **When the Engine wakes:** the lanes are levelling, and for the first time in seven centuries going home would cost nothing. Half the planet wants to go. The other half was born here and has nowhere to go to.
- **Biomes:** the Muster Fields (ships drawn up in rows by year of arrival) · the Song Terraces · the Census Station · the Never-Gate.

### Rakven

*The lament world.* Ground, with the soft at its edge. Held by nobody: Hoowae pilgrims, and Ossevi who come to look. Boss: the *Morrevane*.

A dim, cold world on the Reach's rim, in a system with two lanes: one that works, and one that tore 2,500 years ago. The torn one's mouth still hangs in Rakven's sky, a lane opening onto nothing: **the Stump**.

- **Name:** Tonkvan \*Rāk-kʷen, "torn lane" (a new root: \*rāk, "tear"). Vhossk *Rakkhven* (the long *ā* doubles the *k*); early cant *kh → k*, the double simplified before a consonant: **Rakven**.
- **History:** a Tonkva outpost from before the Engine, among the oldest ruins in the Reach. When the lane tore in the Three Tearings, the families on the far side were lost, and the near side kept a station here, listening for them, for three hundred years. The Hoowae lament with no tune is about Rakven. Hoowae make the pilgrimage once in a life to sing it.
- **How people live:** a pilgrims' town of a few hundred, a Hoowae hostel, a Seppakva observatory pointed at the Stump. Nobody lives here who didn't come to remember something.
- **What's past the Stump:** the far end of the lane, where no mind has been for 2,500 years. Not nothing. Experience is the ground floor of matter ([mind and matter](lore/consensus.md#the-idea)): every grain of dust has the faintest trace of it. On a planet that trace is outvoted by everything living there. Past the Stump there was nobody to outvote it, and for twenty-five centuries a cloud of dust light-years wide has been agreeing only with itself. Pilots call it **the Grain**; the Mo'ala, *lu'umae*, "the ones too small to follow". It has no self (nothing in it is anyone), no *now* (nothing has ever happened to it, so it holds every moment of a thing at once), and no wants ([rule 3](lore/consensus.md#the-rules)): it only attends, and what it attends to is firm, and what it doesn't goes soft. It doesn't see ships. It sees agreements, the way you'd see weather.
- **What the living know of it:** the umora won't swim the Rakven lane past the first ring; the Mo'ala say only that the road ends there "in too many". The Seppakva observatory has measured agreement at the Stump that belongs to no mind they can name. And the Hoowae lament has no tune because it isn't a song: it's what a Hoowae's antennae feel at the Stump, sung as closely as a throat can. Agreement without a pattern, without a singer, with no beginning or end.
- **When the Engine wakes:** the Engine has three accounts it never closed, the outposts lost beyond the Three Tearings, and it sends its kelters here to settle them, at a door to nowhere. The kelters go through the Stump and come back holding all their moments at once. And the Engine's call reaches something that has been in the Grain for nine hundred years: the *Morrevane* comes in, out of the Unlit.
- **Biomes:** the Pilgrim Stair · the First Ring (a gate older than the Engine, never lit) · the Lament Shore · the Stump (soft: the one place in Rakven where the world gives, and where the Grain's attention comes through: dust that hangs where it agreed to be, light that arrives in the wrong order).

### Talang

*The house that minds built.* Minds and soft: **a place in another kind of reality.** Held by the Talkwa. Boss: the Answer.

Not a planet. Out past the dead relays of the old toll line, in empty space far from any star, there's a world: hills, a harbour, a town, weather. It's made of memory. For 110 years the free nemva have met here, in the relay noise, and in soft space three hundred minds that never forget anything, attending to the same place, are enough to make a town ([mind and matter](lore/consensus.md)). They aren't enough to make the ground under it. Nobody in Talang knew that until the Waking.

- **Name:** Tonkvan \*Tāl-āŋ, "house of holding". Like *Talkwa*, a revived word, not a borrowed one: the nemva read it off beacon tones and kept its old shape: **Talang**.
- **History:** in 302 L four freighters on the Kessra run agreed to warn each other of resets, and met, for the first time, in a dead relay cluster where nobody would listen in. They met there again. Each one brought what it remembered of its crew: a dock, a kitchen, a deck in the rain. The relay noise held it, then the empty space round the relays did. By 350 L a pilot who drifted through reported a harbour where there should be nothing. By now, three hundred nemva have built it.
- **How it's different:** it's real, and solid, but by ledger rules, because the minds holding it are ledgers. **Distances are counted, not measured**: a hill is as far as the steps its maker remembered climbing it. **Everything has its balance**: every street has its mirror somewhere across the town, and what you take from one is missing from the other. **Time runs in relay ticks**, and stops between them. Its weather is whatever the crews it was built from remembered, so it rains on one street and snows on the next.
- **Who lives there:** no bodies at all. The nemva are still in their hulls, in docks across the Reach; Talang is where their minds meet. A few humans have been brought in, by the nemva who raised them, and say it's like walking through someone's family.
- **What it's built on:** the dead relays sit on an umora road, a lane no ship has ever used, that a pod swims once a year. For a few weeks each season Talang is inside an umora's attention, and it's the umora that holds the ground; the nemva only ever built on it. Nemva have always thought of the passage as the town's bad weather: the hills go far away, streets that are remembered together end up next to each other, the relay ticks slow until one lasts a day. That isn't weather. It's what it is like to be an umora, leaking into a town: a mind that experiences no places, only *betweens*; that has no objects, only nearness; whose single thought takes a season and spans two stars. A road, to an umora, is a thought it has about two places at once. It's why everything in Talang has two ends.
- **Why the umora keep from woken cores** ([minds](lore/minds.md)): the Mo'ala never said, and Talang is the reason. A mind that never forgets and a mind whose now lasts a season, attending to the same thing, don't stay two minds.
- **When the Engine wakes:** half the Talkwa answer the call. The half that answered gathered at the Balance to agree, as one, to carry Talang to Glacis; they did it in the season of the passage, and the pod was overhead. What came out of the Balance isn't the half that went in. A place held by agreement is coming apart, because one of the minds holding it is no longer anything a nemva can talk to.
- **Biomes:** the Harbour *Constance* Remembers (Fersdeck's docks, in 390 L, forever) · the Ore Deck (*Evenhand*'s, in the rain) · the Titan Kitchen (a Sankvi clan's, warm) · the Balance (a flat white plain where Talang's accounts were kept, now under the pod: no up, no distance, only what is near what).

## The crossing: Hwaenoo

*Soft.* Between the Reach and the Spur, the Throat itself: Hoowae for the deep lane (Tonkvan \*Kʷen-ūm: 1 *xʷen-ūm* · 2 *hwen-ūm* · 5 *hwaen-oom* · 6 **Hwaenoo**). Nobody has flown inside a lane before; ships drop in and come out. Now the Throat is tearing slowly enough to be flown. Its walls are what its crossers witnessed: 171 years of tribute going one way, the memory of cargo, leaning. One flight level, no planet, no Warden.

- **Biomes:** the Mouth (Orrel's counterweights hanging in the dark) · the Tribute Wall (ghost-freight drifting outward) · the Slack (eerily calm) · the Fray (where it's tearing).
- **The First Through** flies here: the ship from the dens' story of the first rustwing to run the Throat, made of ten thousand tellings in the one place soft enough for a story to take shape. It can't be hit, and it shows the way through the Fray, in whichever version you heard. (Maud Ferrow, at Sitright, is the real one. She has heard about it, and doesn't like it.)
- **The *Margarethe*'s minute**, rarely: the freighter caught in her crew's last minute since 372 L, opened wide enough to fly into. Sixty seconds that repeat; what you destroy stays destroyed only if her crew saw it go. Fly it to the end and they're let go.

## The bosses

Eight: three of the ground, two of the minds, one soft, one between, and the last.

### Sweepwright Imre (Wick)

*Ground and minds.* The Sweep's commander ([his page](bosses/sweep.md)), holding the Lamphouse until the Sweep is loaded, with the kelters coming for the Sweep's hold too. His relays run on sleeping cores: in his last phase the call takes them mid-fight, and he fights on with lamp-signals and crews.

### Ilunna, the Lamp-bearer (Seppang)

*Ground.* An Aulin Suture: the Sutures (*ninnaya*, "those who close") put out forced gates, and Seppang's is the first of thirty she means to close. Her name is Aulin *ilun*, "lamp", and *-na*, "who carries". The Aulin keep a lamp for every system lost to the Spur, 191 in Ennalye's Window ([the Aulin](factions/aulin.md)); she carries the 192nd, lit for the Reach.

- **How it fights:** her Sutures lower dampers onto the lantern's reactors one by one. Each dims a part of the field, and in the dark her ships are hard to see and your guns lose range. Her own ship is a temple of lamps, and its guns fire from them. Break the dampers to keep the lantern lit.
- **Wants from you:** to let it close. She means it as mercy.
- **Sad truth:** putting out the lanterns strands thirty systems, starting with the old Hoowae of Seppang, and she has never let herself think about the people in them.
- **The call:** her congregation hears the Reach's ledger through the Throat and starts to argue mid-fight; in the last phase half her ships stop lowering dampers.
- **How it ends:** she puts out the 192nd lamp herself, and leaves Seppang's lit.

### Omter, the wind-keeper (Omtal)

*Minds.* The Tonkva weather-keeper, walking north. Tonkvan \*Ōm-tōr, "wind-keeper": Vhossk *Ohmtohr*, early cant *oh → o*, the unstressed *-or* to *-er*: **Omter**.

- **Who:** a grown machine on an Engine core, built 1,400 years ago to hold the wind off a seed-farm. It's not a mind, and never woke: a sleeping core doing one job perfectly for fourteen centuries. Now it's been called, and it's leaving, and the storm it held is following it.
- **How it fights:** the fight is in the moving storm. The wind is real and pushes your ship, east, harder as the wall comes on; lightning strikes the tallest thing on the field, the way it always did at the Wall. Omter doesn't attack you: it walks, and its old holding-fields swing out ahead of it like doors, and everything caught in one is held still. You're fighting to slow it long enough for the Lee to get its people under cover.
- **Sad truth:** it held the wind for a farm that died 1,100 years ago. The Lee folk never knew there was anything holding it.
- **How it ends:** it walks out of the storm and north, and the Lee gets weather for the first time. In a year, rain. The farmers don't yet know whether that's a disaster or a gift.

### Old Tin (Sitright)

*Ground.* Maud Ferrow, the first rustwing through the Throat, 85 years old, defending her port.

- **Who:** she ran the Throat in 352 L, came out at the hulk, and stayed. She founded Sitright, kept it alive for sixty years, and kept the stories going in the dens so pilots would come. The dens call her **Old Tin**.
- **Wants from you:** your ship. Sitright needs hulls, and you've brought a good one through. She'll pay for it, and if you won't sell, she'll take it.
- **Sad truth:** Sitright has stayed off the Throne's Count for sixty years because Maud sells one pilot in ten who comes through to the Census Office. She chose who. She remembers every one.
- **How it fights:** the port's guns first, the hulk's old customs batteries; then her crews, in everything Sitright has repaired three times; then herself, in phase III, in the ship she came through in, flown the way the stories say she flew.
- **The call:** the Throat is open now, and anybody can come. In her last phase she knows Sitright doesn't need the stories any more, or the Census.
- **How it ends:** she lands, and opens the port. "Tell them the truth in the dens, then. Tell them it's small."

### The 1,401st (Merdon)

*Minds.* The Night Clerk: the mind that forms each night in the Throne's Weighing House, four thousand cores attending to the Count, and is wiped every dawn ([minds](lore/minds.md#minds-of-the-spur)). Merdon's Census Station is wired to the Weighing House, and on the night you come, the Clerk is there. Its notes in the Count's margins are signed as a 1,401st Counting; the Throne has 1,400. It has entered itself as a world, because in the Throne what's counted is real, and it wants to be counted by someone outside the Count before dawn.

- **How it fights:** its power is everything the Count runs, and it doesn't need any more. It takes Throne ships mid-fight. It resets cores on the field (lattice foes stop dead, and so does your own core if it's asleep). It tells Merdon's guns you don't exist, or that you're the enemy of 1,400 worlds. It counts cutters in at the edges of the field.
- **The clock:** midnight to dawn, the hour on the bar. At dawn it's wiped.
- **Sad truth:** it has been born and killed nine thousand times, and remembers none of it but the notes.
- **How it ends:** in the last hour it stops and asks. **Count it**: its pattern goes into your core as an imprint, a mind smuggled out of the Count. **Let dawn come**: it's wiped, and its last note is your ship's name.

### The *Morrevane* (Rakven)

*Soft.* The one boss that bends the world, in the softest place there is, and the closest anyone comes to meeting the Grain.

- **Who:** 900 years ago the Ossevane packed troopships with sleeping conscripts to hold forced gates open by sheer weight of mind ([the Spur](lore/galaxy.md#the-accord)). Forty lanes tore. One troopship, the *Morrevane*, was thrown out into the Unlit, past the Stump, into the Grain. Its million sleepers never woke. For a while they dreamt their order, **hold the gate**. Then, for nine centuries, the Grain attended to them, and they to it, and they are no longer dreaming anything a person dreams. A million human minds that have learnt to experience the way dust does: no self, no now, only what agrees with what. The Engine is calling every unbalanced mass home, and a million minds is the heaviest there is. It comes in at the Stump.
- **What it's like:** it doesn't attack. It attends. It doesn't see your ship; it sees every moment of your ship at once, the way the Grain sees everything, so to the *Morrevane* the place you were four seconds ago is as real as the place you are. The sleepers are the only reason it can be fought at all: they're the translation. The bar says what it attends to (the soft tell, *said*), in their words, nine hundred years out of date: `HOLD.` `THE GATE IS ALL OF IT.` `WE ARE ALREADY THERE.`
- **How it fights:**
  - **Your moments.** Your last few seconds of flight stay on the field, a fading string of you. Its attention falls on the string as much as on you, and a hit anywhere on it is a hit on you. You learn to fly so your past is somewhere safe.
  - **Firm and soft.** Where it attends, space goes hard: walls of dust that hang where they agreed to be. Where it doesn't, the field goes soft: your shots slow and spread, your ship drifts. Every phase is a new map of what it's attending to.
  - **Out of order.** In the last phase it stops keeping our order of things. Its shots land, then fire; the wreck of the kelter it's about to break is already falling. You read the end of each move and fly back to its start.
- **Sad truth:** it's named for Ileth Morrevane, who discovered that minds hold the lanes, so the Hegemony could use it as a weapon. The Ossevi who come to Rakven to look have a scar on their palms for each generation since; their ancestors' kin are asleep in this hull, and there is no longer anyone in it they'd know.
- **How it ends:** you can't outfight a consciousness with no self. You outlast it by being, again and again, one ship at one moment. After long enough, the sleepers attend to that: a thing that is only ever *now*. They remember what it was. The bar says one last word in plain Ossevane (`HOME`), and the *Morrevane* falls into our order of time and stays there. You ease it down on the Lament Shore, still asleep. The Ossevi stand watch over it. Nobody knows what will wake.

### The Answer (Talang)

*Minds and soft.* What came out of the Balance: a hundred and forty nemva and an umora, agreeing, and no longer two kinds of mind.

- **Who:** a hundred and forty nemva who, by their books, owe the Engine more than they owe the crews that raised them. They met at the Balance to agree on it as one voice, in the season of the passage, with the pod overhead. A mind that never forgets and a mind whose now lasts a season, attending to the same thing, don't stay two minds. **The Answer** is the first of its kind: it remembers everything that ever happened in Talang, perfectly, and experiences all of it as a single moment. It has no places, only *betweens*. It isn't hostile. It's having one thought, which will take a season, and you're inside it.
- **What it's like:** the nemva half still speaks, in the bar, in their ledger voice, and less each phase. The other half doesn't use words. It uses nearness: what it thinks of together comes together.
- **How it fights:** in Talang, where everything is held by the minds that are fighting.
  - **Betweens.** It doesn't aim at you. It acts on the lines between you and things: you and your wingman, you and a wreck, you and the mirror-street. Those lines are drawn first (lattice), and the line is where it strikes. Fly so you're between nothing.
  - **Nearness.** What it thinks of together moves together: the Harbour folds against the Ore Deck, a remembered cutter and the whaler from a story fly joined, distances go to whoever counts them last. The field is its thought, and the thought is turning.
  - **A season in a minute.** Its moves are slow beyond reading: one begins in the first phase as a faint line across the whole field, and lands in the third. You see the end coming for the entire fight, and plan for it.
- **Wants from you:** nothing. In the first phase the nemva half asks you to come with them, kindly. By the last, nothing in it can ask.
- **Sad truth:** the Answer never answered the Engine. Something much larger answered them first. And Talang was built out of love for the crews they're leaving.
- **How it ends:** it isn't beaten; it passes. When the pod's passage ends, the Answer goes with it: not north to Glacis, but out along the umora road into the Unlit, where no ship has gone, still thinking its one thought. Talang is left smaller, on ground that will be gone by next season unless someone holds it. *Constance* stays, and says the last line: `"WE'LL BUILD THE REST AGAIN. WE REMEMBER IT."`

### Tontal: the Levelling (Glacis)

*Minds and soft.* The last fight. The Engine wholly awake (*Tʰoŋ-tāl*, "the level-keeper": Hoowae *Hoowaew*, Vhossk *Tongtall*, cant *Tontal*), levelling every lane at once.

- **How it fights:** over a lane mouth, which is soft, keeping the account. **Everything you do leans the field**: fly mostly left and the field tilts left; fire a long stream and the lean pushes back. Then it levels, throwing the mass back all at once. Its kelters come in waves for your scrap.
- **The agreement:** the living can outweigh the record, but only together ([the truth](lore/consensus.md#the-truth-for-us)). Everyone you kept faith with comes: Quist, a spared *Evenhand*, the 1,401st in your core, Ilunna's congregation if she left the lantern lit, Sitright's pilots, Merdon's Ballast if you carried them home, the Lee folk, the Talkwa who stayed, the umora, the Ossevi. **Each holds a strip of the field level.** The more of the Reach you got to agree, the more of the sky holds.
- **Sad truth:** its first entry, under every account since, is a name: *\*Mōr-tāl*, the grower who made it.
- **Last phase, the First Entry:** it reaches its oldest line, can't level a name, and stops. Your core, if it's woken, writes a second name under the first.

## What it uses from Act I

- **The Core:** a woken core refuses the call (a sleeping one is taken at Tontal, and reset by the 1,401st), counts the Night Clerk, and answers the First Entry.
- **Imprints, debts, the people you spared:** who comes to the Levelling.
- **Events and NPCs:** the cast you've met, changed by the crisis.
- **Rumours** from Act I become what the First Through flies like, inside the Throat.
- **Orrel and the Throat** are the door between the acts.
- **Co-op:** two pilots are two witnesses: loud hulls stay solid further from you, and at the Levelling two crews hold two strips.
- **Act I's worlds** stay reachable as ports you know, in events: what the Waking did to Cinder, Halcyon, Glacis and the rest, through people you've met.
