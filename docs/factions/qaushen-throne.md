# The Qaushen Throne

*A thousand worlds run as one great census: the safest place in the Spur to be born, and the least free. It wrote the Spur's highest law, and breaks it every day in the Reach.* Pilots call it **the Koshen**; Compact hymns, **the Cawshen**; its own ledgers, **the Count**. A [galactic power](README.md#galactic-powers). The wider Spur: [lore](../lore/galaxy.md). How it's told: [tone](../lore/tone.md).

## What it is, and what it can't admit

The Throne began as a way of making sure nobody starved, and it still does that better than anyone: every counted subject has bread, a roof, a posting and a number, and when a Counting's harvest fails, the Count moves grain to it within the season. Famines happen in the Throne; they don't last. People love it for that, and most of its subjects would never live anywhere else. The price is that everything is counted, everything is weighed, and when the lanes lean the Throne levels them with people: conscripts and colonists shipped to the margins as ballast, for life.

What it can't admit is that its Count, the thing it believes holds the empire real, now has a lie in it. The Reach is entered as the Margin of the Uncounted, reached through a Tonkva gate reopened level, its lanterns all Tonkva-built; in fact every gate in it is forced, the Throat worst of all, and the tribute that crosses it leans it further every year. To make the books balance, house Qaurin enters counterweight shipments back through the Throat that never sailed. By the Zhaun's own doctrine a false count, held by billions, *weighs*: the Throne is making a piece of its world less true every year, and the house doing it knows.

## History

- **~1,920 BP. The First Count.** Nine small Zhaun systems, the Nine Holdings, whose lanes kept leaning and whose grain kept arriving short. A clerk-priest, **Ishaun of the Granaries**, counted every sack that crossed for a year, and moved what had to move to make it even. Nobody starved that winter. She was made the first **Weigher**, and the census was the government from then on.
- **~1,900–1,500 BP. The Long Counting.** The Throne offered its neighbours a count: their lanes levelled, their harvests guaranteed, in return for being counted. Hungry worlds took it. Proud ones didn't, and the Throne fought them, slowly, patiently, sometimes for a century; it numbers those wars rather than naming them, and there are sixty-one. Humans were counted in during these centuries, from the worlds along what is now the Tear, and became its clerks, engineers and soldiers.
- **~1,450 BP. The Weighing House.** The census outgrew its clerks. Weigher **Zhaurem the Patient** bought three hundred cores from the Tonkva through the Throat and wired them into a counting-hall at Qaun-Ashel. It has grown ever since, and the Throne has wanted the machine behind the cores, the Engine, from that day.
- **~950–900 BP. The Ossevane War.** The Ossevane Hegemony fought the Throne for the Spur and, losing, forced its own gates with troopships full of sleeping minds. The Throne won by refusing to do the same. After the Sundering it wrote the **Accord of Level** (897 BP), and every power signed. The proudest thing the Throne has ever done.
- **~760 BP. The Ballast Reforms.** Weigher **Meirun the Second** ended the Throne's last free migrations and made the army the Counterweight: every subject's twentieth year drawn by lot, twenty years' service at the far end of a lane, and no road home after (bringing them back would undo the weight). Most marry where they serve. It levelled the empire's lanes for seven centuries. It is the most hated law in the Throne.
- **~692 BP. Ostrand.** The human forge-world rose against the draft and forced a gate to get its children away. The Throne crushed it and sent its leaders into the **Long Dark**, at the Uncounted. (They lived. See [the Compact](halcyon-compact.md).)
- **171 BP (241 L). The Opening.** The exiles' grandchildren forced the Throat from the Reach side, and the Fourteenth Weight came through. The Pan of Margins saw an Engine, and decided to keep it quiet.
- **141 BP (271 L). The Night of Two Counts.** At dawn the Weighing House handed up two ledgers instead of one. The second counted the Counterweight as people, by name, instead of as mass. Nobody had asked for it. The Pan of Census reset the House by noon, and the next year the Weigher signed the **Clean Count** (272 L): a woken core is a fault, everywhere, and must be wiped. The second ledger was burnt. One copy wasn't.

## The Count is real

The Zhaun believe what is counted is **real**: the census is a witnessing, billions of subjects held in one great act of attention, and that is what keeps the empire firm ([mind and matter](../lore/consensus.md)). It overclaims (worlds hold themselves), but counted lanes are travelled lanes, and travelled lanes stay wide, so the doctrine has never been disproved. To be *uncounted* is, to a Zhaun, to be less than real. That's what the Throne meant when it named the Reach.

## How it's run

- **The Weigher** is the sovereign, chosen for life by the Scale from among the eldest Zhaun. The Weigher's power is to **sign the Count**: no tax, war or law is real until it's in the year's ledger over the Weigher's mark.
- **The Scale** is the council of twelve ministries, called **Pans**: the Pan of Lanes, of Census, of Counterweight (the army), of Margins (the frontiers), of Tribute, of Law, and six more. Each Pan is a Zhaun house's for generations. Imperial politics is the war between the Pans.
- **Countings** are the provinces, each run by a **qaunar** (a counter) with a census office, a tribute office and a garrison.
- **Margins** are the frontiers, run by a **Doshen** (Court *daushen*, "of the outer lands"), who has a soldier's powers and answers to the Pan of Margins alone.
- **The Weighing House**: four thousand cores in a hall at Qaun-Ashel, keeping the Count, **reset every night** so none of them wakes. (Before 272 L it was wiped once a year, which was thought plenty: a lone core takes twenty years to wake. Four thousand wired together, all attending to the same thing, turned out to take much less.) The Throne's whole government rests on minds it wipes daily, and the Pan of Census knows exactly why: a waking House would have its own opinion of what's real. It already did, once.

## How people live

- **Every subject is counted.** Each has a census number, long enough to count hundreds of billions; in daily use people give only its last part, their place in their own street's register, as a second name: Iselde Seven-Hundred-Ninth. To be uncounted is not to exist.
- **The Standing.** Once a year, at dawn on Count Day, every subject stands still at their own door for one minute while they're counted. Each world stands at its own dawn, so the Standing rolls round a world in a day and across the empire over a season. Children love it; the old weep. It's the one thing the whole empire attends to, and the Zhaun believe it's what holds it together for the year.
- **The lot.** The Throne levels its lanes with people, because a person leans a lane far more than their body's weight: a mind crossing witnesses the lane as it is witnessed ([mind and matter](../lore/consensus.md)). Freight goes in; people go out. At twenty, every subject's name goes into the Counterweight draw. One in nine is sent. Families keep a chair empty for them at the Standing.
- **The Ballast.** The margins are full of the drawn: garrisons, colonies and penal worlds of people who will never go home, and who have become peoples of their own, with their own songs about the lane that took them. The Fourteenth Weight on the Throat is Ballast.
- **The Zhaun** are one in ten thousand of the Throne. Tall, slow, long-lived (about six hundred years), with **bone-white face-plates** grown from the skull, smooth and expressionless; their feelings travel by scent, which other peoples can't read. A Zhaun among other peoples is always a little alone.

## Inside

- **The Pan of Margins** (house Qaurin) runs the Reach as its own secret, and the Reach's cores have made it the richest Pan. Its whole fortune is the lie.
- **The Pan of Lanes** (house Zhaivar) suspects. If the Reach comes out, Margins falls, and Lanes takes everything it held.
- **The Pan of Counterweight** wants the Engine for the kindest reason in the Throne: the day it has a machine to level the lanes, it can stop sending people away.
- **The Pan of Census** (house Zhaurel) keeps the Weighing House, and the Clean Count, and the burnt ledger's secret.
- **The Quiet Numbers**: clerks across the Countings, most of them human, who believe a Count that leaves people out isn't a Count. They do nothing illegal. They just keep very careful records, and share them.

## People

- **Ashqaur IV, the Weigher.** Four hundred and ten, ninety years on the throne: gentle, meticulous, and the last Weigher who signs every page of the Count himself. He wants to die with the Count balanced. He signed the Margin into the Count as level, on the Pan of Margins' word, and believes it. He's going blind, and for the last six years his hand has been guided on the page.
- **Iselde Seven-Hundred-Ninth, the Weigher's Hand.** A human clerk who reads him every page and guides his pen, and so decides, in a way, what's real. She has noticed that the Margins entries for the Uncounted have been written by the same hand for 171 years. She is a Quiet Number, and has started writing to an Ottexa clerk.
- **Iru Qaurin, Doshen of the Throat.** The envoy's son (the envoy, Zhaimel, is alive at three hundred and ninety, and heads the Pan of Margins at Qaun-Ashel), a Zhaun who has lived sixty years on a fortress-station among ten thousand humans who can't read his scent. He rules from the Throat and is rarely seen. His family's fortune is the lie he keeps. Nobody can tell that he has come to love the Reach: he keeps Oru-Tal ferns in his quarters, and has read every issue of the Lantern Gazette.
- **Weight-Captain Tamas Orrel**, of the Fourteenth Weight. An Ossevi, with thirty-one scars across his palms, one for every generation since his people forced their gates and tore forty lanes. He commands the garrison of a forced gate, and knows exactly what that means. His soldiers are Ballast who will never go home; he's their father, and they know it.
- **Lelaun Zhaivar, Pan of Lanes.** Charming, patient, two hundred and forty, and quite prepared to set the Spur on fire to ruin house Qaurin. Lanes has an agent in the Reach. Nobody knows who.

## The Throne and the Reach

- **Before the Hush** the Throne traded with the Tonkva through the Throat, and coveted the Engine.
- **241 L. The Opening.** The Fourteenth Weight came through within a month.
- **246 L.** The Throne's envoy, **Zhaimel Qaurin** of the Pan of Margins, dictated the Treaty of Nine Wells, chartered the Syndicate under the **Writ of the Throat**, and became the first Doshen of the Throat.
- **246 L to now.** Tribute out through the Throat: crossed metal, umora oil, and **Engine cores**, above all, for the Throne's own attempt to grow an Engine. The Spur is told the Throat was reopened level, and believes it.
- **In the Reach now:** the Doshen and the Fourteenth Weight on the Throat; the **Census Office at Fersdeck**, counting the Compact, which hates it; imperial merchants at Bluepalm and Refinery Nine, buying cores.

## Friends and enemies

It wrote the Accord, so it is every power's nominal ally against forcers, and the [Aulin](aulin.md) watch it for hypocrisy. The [Ottexa](ottexa.md) keeps its master books, and has kept a great deal else. The [Long Herd](long-herd.md) won't speak to it. In the Reach it owns the [Syndicate](syndicate.md), rules the [Compact](halcyon-compact.md) through its old exile, buys from the [Uukhvar](dune-cartel.md) and [Vantorr](vantorr.md), and is hated by almost everyone with a memory.

## The Waking

The Engine waking would level the Throat: pull back 171 years of tribute from the Throne's side. It would unlock the Throat as a Tonkva gate, and its beacons would read the Reach's ledger out into the Spur. And when it calls every sleeping core, the Weighing House will answer it. The Pan of Margins has one plan: hold the Engine before it finishes waking. Its fleet is gathering behind the Throat. The Pan of Counterweight is quietly hoping it fails.
