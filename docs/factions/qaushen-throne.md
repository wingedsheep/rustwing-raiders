# The Qaushen Throne

*The empire next door: a thousand worlds run as one great census, which wrote the Spur's highest law and breaks it every day in the Reach.* Pilots call it **the Koshen**; Compact hymns, **the Cawshen**; its own ledgers, **the Count**. A [galactic power](README.md#galactic-powers). The wider Spur: [lore](../lore/galaxy.md).

## Origin

The Throne began as a census. Nearly two thousand years ago the **Zhaun**, a long-lived, slow-breeding people with few children and long memories, ruled a small cluster of systems whose lanes kept leaning. With no machine to keep them level, they did it by hand: they counted every cargo, every ship and every person that crossed, and moved what had to move to keep the count even. The counting needed clerks; the clerks needed soldiers; the soldiers needed worlds to feed them. Two thousand years later the census is an empire, and it still works the same way.

## How it's run

- **The Weigher** is the sovereign, chosen for life by the Scale from among the eldest Zhaun. Ashqaur IV is four hundred and ten years old, and has reigned for ninety. The Weigher's power is to **sign the Count**: no tax, war or law is real until it's in the year's ledger over the Weigher's mark.
- **The Scale** is the council of twelve ministries, called **Pans**: the Pan of Lanes, of Census, of Counterweight (the army), of Margins (the frontiers), of Tribute, of Law, and six more. Each Pan is a Zhaun house's for generations. Imperial politics is the war between the Pans.
- **Countings** are the provinces, each run by a **qaunar** (a counter) with a census office, a tribute office and a garrison.
- **Margins** are the frontiers, run by a **Doshen** (Court *daushen*, "of the outer lands"), who has a soldier's powers and answers to the Pan of Margins alone.
- **Every subject is counted.** Each has a census number, and in the Throne's own worlds people use it as a second name: Iselde Seven-Hundred-Ninth. To be **uncounted** is to not exist. The Reach's imperial name, the Uncounted, was a judgement.

## The Count is real

The Zhaun believe what is counted is **real**: the census is a witnessing, billions of subjects held in one great act of attention, and that is what keeps the empire firm ([mind and matter](../lore/consensus.md)). It overclaims (worlds hold themselves), but counted lanes are travelled lanes, and travelled lanes stay wide, so the doctrine has never been disproved. To be *uncounted* is, to a Zhaun, to be less than real. That's what the Throne meant when it named the Reach.

## The Weighing House

The census is too big for clerks alone. For most of its history the Throne has kept it with **cores**, bought from the Tonkva before the Hush: today four thousand of them, wired together in the Weighing House at Qaun-Ashel, with more arriving from the Reach every year. Cores learn, and cores left alone wake ([minds](../lore/minds.md)), so the Weighing House is **reset every night**. The **Clean Count** (272 L) makes it law everywhere: a woken core is a fault and must be wiped. The Throne's whole government rests on minds it kills daily, and the Pan of Census knows exactly why: four thousand cores attending to the Count are a great weight of witness, and it must all agree with the Weigher. A Weighing House that woke would have its own opinion of what's real.

## The Counterweight

The Throne's answer to the lean is people. Tribute flows inward to the core worlds, and to level the lanes the Throne sends mass back outward: conscripts, colonists, convicts and whole garrisons, shipped to the margins and never brought home. The army is literally called **the Counterweight** (Court *meirdaun*, "weight sent"): twenty-year service, at the far end of the lane. In the Throne a soldier is ballast, and knows it. Its legions are named by number: **Weights**.

## The Zhaun

Tall, slow and quiet. Their one feature: **bone-white face-plates** grown from the skull, smooth and expressionless; Zhaun feeling is carried in scent, which other peoples can't read. They live about six hundred years and are perhaps one in ten thousand of the Throne's subjects. Everyone else serves: humans most of all, the most numerous subject people in the Throne, and its clerks, soldiers and engineers.

## The Throne and the Reach

- **Before the Hush** the Throne traded with the Tonkva through the Throat, and coveted the Engine.
- **~280 years before the Landing.** The forge-world **Ostrand**, a human Counting, rose against the Counterweight draft, and forced a gate to get its people away. The Throne crushed it, and sentenced its leaders and their families to the **Long Dark**: three sleeper arks aimed at the Uncounted. (They lived. See [the Compact](halcyon-compact.md).)
- **241 L. The Opening.** The Ostranders' descendants forced the Throat. The Throne's **Fourteenth Weight** came through within a month.
- **246 L.** The Throne's envoy, **Zhaimel Qaurin** of the Pan of Margins, dictated the Treaty of Nine Wells, chartered the Syndicate under the **Writ of the Throat**, and became the first Doshen of the Throat.
- **246 L to now.** Tribute out through the Throat: crossed metal, umora oil, and **Engine cores**, above all, for the Throne's own attempt to grow an Engine. The rest of the Spur is told the Uncounted is still dead.

## In the Reach now

- **The Doshen of the Throat**: **Iru Qaurin**, the envoy's great-grandson, a Zhaun of the Pan of Margins, who rules from the fortress-station on the Throat and is rarely seen.
- **The Fourteenth Weight**: the garrison of the Throat, ten thousand conscripts who know they will never go home. Their fortress holds the Throat's Reach end.
- **The Census Office at Fersdeck**, counting the Compact, which hates it.
- **Imperial merchants** at Bluepalm and Refinery Nine, buying cores.

## Inside: the Pans at war

- **The Pan of Margins** (house Qaurin) runs the Reach as its own secret, and the Reach's cores have made it the richest Pan. If the Weigher has been told the whole truth, the Weigher has never signed it into the Count.
- **The Pan of Lanes** (house Ashqaur's rivals) suspects. If the Reach comes out, Margins falls, and Lanes takes everything it held.
- **The Pan of Counterweight** wants the Engine, because the day the Throne has one, it can stop sending people away.

## Friends and enemies

It wrote the Accord, so it is every power's nominal ally against forcers, and the [Aulin](aulin.md) watch it for hypocrisy. The [Ottexa](ottexa.md) keeps its master books. The [Long Herd](long-herd.md) won't speak to it. In the Reach it owns the [Syndicate](syndicate.md), rules the [Compact](halcyon-compact.md) through its old exile, buys from the [Uukhvar](dune-cartel.md) and [Vantorr](vantorr.md), and is hated by almost everyone with a memory.

## The Waking

The Engine waking would level the Throat: pull back 171 years of tribute from the Throne's side. And it would unlock the Throat properly, as a Tonkva gate, and its beacons would read the Reach's ledger out into the Spur, where anyone could hear it. The Pan of Margins has one plan: hold the Engine before it finishes waking. Its fleet is gathering behind the Throat.
