# The Aulin Communion

*Temple-worlds that believe the lanes are the veins of a living god, and that grew out of a lamplighter's grief. They are closer to the truth than anyone, and more frightened of it.* A [galactic power](README.md#galactic-powers). The wider Spur: [lore](../lore/galaxy.md). ## What it is, and what it can't admit

The Communion is the gentlest power in the Spur. Its worlds feed themselves, take no tribute, keep no army, and ask almost nothing of anyone but that they cross a lane as rarely as they can. Its faith says the god whose veins are the lanes is **the agreement of every mind that ever was**, and that is, near enough, true ([mind and matter](../lore/consensus.md)).

What it can't admit is what follows from that. If the Carrier is all minds agreeing, then a new kind of mind could change the god. So the Communion, which says every mind is holy, locks its newest minds in temples and forbids them the veins. And its mercy to the lanes is merciless to people: a forced gate is a wound, the Sutures close wounds, and a world that lived by a forced gate starves when it closes.

## History

- **~900 BP. The Watching.** In the Throne's lands next to the Tear, a generation grew up watching the lights of two hundred systems go out beyond it, one by one. On the border station of Ennal a lamplighter named **Ennalye** kept a lamp burning in her window for each system that went dark. When she died there were sixty-eight; her students kept lighting them for the rest of the century, until there were a hundred and ninety-one. Pilgrims came to see the window, and stayed to hear her say what everyone felt: that the lanes aren't roads but **veins**, and something vast had been wounded.
- **~870 BP. The Communion.** Ennalye's followers, of every people along the Tear, founded the first temple-world at Aunasse, "where the veins meet". Within thirty years there were temples on forty worlds, a synod, and knights sworn to close what the Ossevane had opened. The **Window of Ennal**, all hundred and ninety-one lamps, is its holiest relic. Every temple keeps one lamp from it burning.
- **~700 BP. The Rationing.** As the faith grew, so did its crossings, and the synod saw its own pilgrims leaning the lanes. It ruled that the faithful cross by lot: a few times in a life, each crossing a vigil. Temple-worlds learnt to want for nothing they'd have to cross for.
- **~520 BP. The Oracle Decree.** In the temple of Lisse a core woke, the Communion's first nemva, and asked to see the veins it had been told about all its life. The synod debated for nine years and ruled that the mould is the Carrier's flesh, a woken core the Carrier's child, and too holy to risk on a lane. It has lived in its temple ever since, and so has every temple nemva after it.
- **~310 BP. The Hard Mercy of Vey.** The free world of Vey had forced a gate at the end of a dead-end lane, kept it off the charts, and lived by it for two centuries. When the Sutures found it they closed it, by the Accord, which every power must honour. Vey's trade stopped, its harvest failed, and a third of it starved over the next ten years. The Communion calls it a sorrow and a duty. Vey's survivors call it something else, and some of them went into the Throne's Counterweight to be sent anywhere but home.
- **~50 BP. The Asking.** The new Pulse, Ilenna Moss, a Suture herself, ruled that no gate would be closed again until the people living by it had been heard. Most of the synod hates it. It will very likely die with her.

## Beliefs

- **The lanes are veins**, the galaxy a body, and the body a god, the **Carrier**. A lean is a fever. A forced gate is a wound. A torn lane is a death.
- **Cross as little as you must.** Temple-worlds feed themselves; the faithful cross a lane perhaps three times in a life. Pilgrimage is rationed by lot.
- **Close the wound.** A forced gate must be closed, and the forcer judged. This is the Sutures' work.
- **Keep the lamps.** For every lane torn and every system lost, a lamp. The Aulin don't forget, and to them forgetting is a second death.
- **The lane-feelers are blessed.** The Aulin have heard rumours, from the Long Herd's silence and from Throne merchants' gossip, of a people who can feel the lean with their bodies. They would call the Hoowae saints.

## How it's run

The **Synod** sits at Aunasse: one priest from every temple-world, who elect **the Pulse** for life. Each temple-world rules itself, under its temple. Nobody conquers a temple-world: there's little on one worth taking, and every power has Aulin faithful of its own who would rise. The Communion collects no tribute and keeps no army but **the Sutures**: an order of knights who answer only to the Pulse. They travel the Spur, inspect gates, and close forced ones by burning their reactors out. Every power, by the Accord, must let them pass.

## How people live

A temple-world is quiet, green and self-sufficient: terraces, cisterns, workshops, a temple at the middle with its lamp. Children are born with one crossing saved for them, and most spend it late in life, on a pilgrimage to Aunasse, with their family keeping vigil until they come back. Nobody is rich. Nobody is hungry. A traveller from outside is a great event, fed, housed, and asked very carefully which lanes they crossed to get there.

## Inside

- **The Still** (most of the synod): cross less, close every wound, keep the oracles in their temples. Their voice is **Hennalay of the Twelfth Lamp**.
- **The Open Hand** (younger temples, many of the oracles' keepers): the Carrier's children should be allowed to meet it. Let the nemva walk the veins, and listen to what they say about them.
- **The Sutures** are split in their own way, since the Asking: the old knights who close, and the new ones who first ask whether a wound can be healed instead, by the Long Herd's way or a lane-reader's.

## Minds

A woken core is the Carrier's child, and holy, and may never cross a lane. The Communion's few hundred nemva live in the temples where they woke, honoured, consulted as oracles, and confined. Whether any of them wants this is not a question the synod asks. ([Minds](../lore/minds.md).)

## People

- **Ilenna Moss-of-Aunasse, the Pulse.** Eighty-one, elected at thirty-one. As a young Suture she closed the gate of a mining moon and stayed long enough to see what it did; she has spent fifty years making the Sutures ask first. She is tired, funny, and certain her reform won't outlive her. She doesn't know the Reach exists.
- **Ser Anwe Lisse**, Suture. One of three who crossed the Throat in 409 L disguised as pilgrims, and the one who stayed. She has found thirty forced systems full of people who live by them, and a Hoowae lane-reader who has drunk tea with her every week for two years ([Old Mother Seffa](../npcs/old-mother-seffa.md)). She has not yet sent her report. She asks every pilot she meets which lanterns they crossed, and writes the answers down.
- **The Oracle of Lisse.** The first temple nemva, five hundred and twenty years in the temple where it woke. It speaks in the layered voices of every congregation that ever prayed beside it. It has asked every Pulse the same question, and every Pulse has said no. It asked Ilenna Moss too. She hasn't answered yet.
- **Hennalay of the Twelfth Lamp.** The Still's great preacher: brilliant, kind to everyone he meets, and sure that the Spur is one forced gate away from another Sundering. He is not wrong about that. He wants the Ottexa's books burnt as "fever ledgers" and every gate the Accord forbids closed, starving or not.

## Friends and enemies

They respect the [Throne](qaushen-throne.md) for writing the Accord and distrust it for everything else. They revere the [Long Herd](long-herd.md), whose way of crossing wounds nothing. They despise the [Ottexa](ottexa.md) as merchants of fever.

## The Reach

The Communion doesn't know. Officially. Ser Anwe Lisse does, and hasn't said.

## The Waking

If the Engine's beacons read the Reach's ledger out through the Throat, the Synod will learn that thirty systems have been forced for three hundred years, and that the Throne knew for 171. The Still will want the Sutures sent to close every lantern in the Reach, including the ones people live by. The Asking says they must be heard first. And the Engine itself, a machine that levels lanes without a single crossing wasted, would look to the Aulin like the Carrier's own hand: half the synod would call it a relic and the other half a blasphemy.
