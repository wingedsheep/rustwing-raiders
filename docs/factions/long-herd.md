# The Long Herd

*Nomads who live in the wake of the lane-drifters, cross without gates, and have known the Reach was alive for a thousand years, and said nothing.* A [galactic power](README.md#galactic-powers). The wider Spur: [lore](../lore/galaxy.md). How it's told: [tone](../lore/tone.md).

## What it is, and what it can't admit

The Long Herd is the Spur's living proof that you can cross without leaning anything: follow the umora, close in a pod's wake, and the lane barely notices you. It is older than the Throne, owns nothing, rules no one, and has never fought a war it could walk away from.

What it can't admit is that its answer only works for people willing to give up everything, and that its oldest virtue is a kind of withdrawal. *Never speak of a calving ground to anyone who might go there* kept the umora safe for longer than anyone can date. It also meant the Mo'ala watched the Tonkva dwindle through the Hush and told nobody, and it means that today the Lowtide Court is killing their herds' eldest at Vesh and doesn't know the Herd exists.

## History

- **Before anyone counted. The First Following.** The Mo'ala tell it as one night: a world of theirs (they won't say which) where a pod of umora came down so low over the sea that the people could hear the shells ring, and a girl sounded back on her throat-sac, and the pod waited for her. They left their world in the pod's wake and never owned one again. Nobody outside the Herd knows if any of it happened.
- **~2,000 BP. The Tonkva.** Mo'ala pod-riders met the Tonkva at Vesh, and taught them the word for the great slow ones; the Tonkva took it home and misheard it as *umora*. The Herd thought gates rude and harmless: they kept their lanes level, so the pods didn't mind them.
- **~1,160 BP. The Hush.** Every gate in the Reach locked. Only the umora kept coming, and the Mo'ala with them. For a thousand years the riders saw the Reach alive: the Tonkva's outposts dwindling to Hoowae, the Vhossk keeping their markets, the bloom growing. The first law held. They said nothing.
- **~1,050 BP. Wa'ilu's Telling.** The lane-surveyor Ileth Morrevane learnt from an old Mo'ala rider, **Wa'ilu**, that the umora's minds hold the roads open. She published, and the Spur's science of witness began ([mind and matter](../lore/consensus.md#how-it-was-found)). Wa'ilu broke no law: he spoke of the roads, not of a calving ground. But the Herd watched what the powers did with it (the Crowded Fleets, the Sundering), and made its second law: no rider speaks to a power; only a **Herd-Speaker** does, and a Herd-Speaker binds no one.
- **~950 BP. The Taking of Three Herds.** The Ossevane tried to conscript the Herd: herd-ships to lead its troop fleets through lanes in the pods' wake. The herds scattered. Three were caught, their pods harpooned to make them go where the fleets wanted, and the pods died in the lanes, and the herds with them. The Mo'ala sang them for a hundred years. It is why no herd will ever travel with a power again.
- **~30 BP to now. The Thinning.** The pods coming back from the Reach have been smaller each year, and the old bulls missing.

## The Mo'ala

Long-limbed, dark-skinned, slow and soft-spoken in air. Their feature: **a throat-sac** that sounds low calls, felt more than heard, which is how they speak to the umora and to each other through a hull. They live on herd-ships built for the long drift, in families of a few hundred.

## How people live

A herd's life is a migration: decades-long circuits of the Spur behind one pod, and the herd is named for the pod's eldest bull. The Herd owns no world, but it can't live without them: it puts in at free ports along the pods' road to trade umora-calls on bone, passage in a wake and lane-sense for hull plate, parts and seed, and grows its food in the plankton the pods feed on. A herd-ship is old, patched and loved; a new one is built once in a generation, at a yard that has served the same herd for centuries. Children are born mid-lane and named for where the pod was when they came. Nobody hurries. Songs are sung on the throat-sac and felt through the deck by everyone aboard. The dead are given to the lane: let out into the pod's wake, the only burial in the Spur that crosses. A Mo'ala who leaves the Herd to live on a world is mourned as if dead, and welcomed back as if born.

## How it's run

No government but **the Gather**: whenever the pods meet, every herd-ship sends one voice, and they talk until they agree, which can take a season. Each migration they choose a **Herd-Speaker** to talk to outsiders, who has no power to bind anyone. Mo'ala law is short, and the first law is the oldest: **never speak of a calving ground to anyone who might go there.**

## The dreamers of the roads

The umora's minds are what hold the lanes open ([mind and matter](../lore/consensus.md)). The Mo'ala always knew. To the Long Herd, killing an umora isn't hunting: it's unmaking a road. And a herd that loses its bull doesn't only grieve: it loses its way, and drifts.

## Inside

- **The Old Wake** (most of the herds): keep the first law. The pods will decide; they always have.
- **The Loud Herds** (the young, and the herds that lost their bulls): break the law, tell the Spur what is happening at Vesh, and go in through the Throat to stop the hunt themselves. Their speaker is **Haa'loma**.

## People

- **Ume'ilo Wa'anu, Herd-Speaker.** Very old, with no power to bind anyone and forty years of speaking to outsiders who never quite listen. Her own herd followed the bull **Ume'ayo**, the eldest umora any rider knew, into the Reach nineteen years ago. The pod came back without him. Her herd has been drifting ever since. She doesn't know that Ume'ayo is the Throne-Shell, and that Queen Istra rules from inside his shell ([the Lowtide Court](reef-pirates.md)).
- **Lo'oma**, pod-rider. Young, quick, and the Mo'ala the Eefee of Vesh know: she rides a pod into the Reach each season and out again. She has watched the Court's harpooners at work from a hundred yards off, and once, from closer, Istra herself. She knows the Throne-Shell is Ume'ayo (you don't mistake the eldest bull's scars) and hasn't told the Herd-Speaker, who is her great-aunt. She has broken the first law in small ways already: an Eefee friend, a rustwing who gave her a tow.
- **Haa'loma of the Loud Herds.** Lo'oma's uncle, whose herd lost its bull twelve years ago. Fierce, grieving, and ready to break the oldest law there is.

## Their way of crossing

To the Spur's powers the Mo'ala are the only people who cross without gates, tolls or lean. The Throne has tried for centuries to hire, tax and conscript them, and gets nowhere: the herds simply go where the pods go. The [Aulin](aulin.md) revere them. Everyone else trades with them warily, for umora-calls recorded on bone and passage in a pod's wake.

## The Reach

- **The umora calve at Vesh**, in the plankton haze over the lagoon, and come back there to die. For the Long Herd the Reach is the most sacred place in the Spur.
- A few Mo'ala ride the pods into the Reach each season. The Eefee of Vesh know them; the [Lowtide Court](reef-pirates.md) doesn't.
- **The umora aren't coming back.** The Gather knows why: the Court's hunt, and the Throne's market for its oil.

## Friends and enemies

They speak to the [Aulin](aulin.md), trade with the [Ottexa](ottexa.md), and won't speak to the [Throne](qaushen-throne.md). The [Lowtide Court](reef-pirates.md) is killing their herds, and doesn't know they exist.

## The Waking

The umora hear the Engine first. The pods are leaving the lanes it will level. The Gather is debating, for the first time in its history, breaking the first law; the Loud Herds won't wait for it to finish.
