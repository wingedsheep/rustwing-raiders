# Mind and matter

What the Spur learnt about reality: that mind isn't a passenger in the universe but part of what holds it together, and that where minds agree, the world is firmer. This page has the idea, its rules, how it was found, what each power makes of it, and the paths it opens. Overview: [lore](README.md). The lanes it explains: [how the lanes work](lanes-and-gates.md). The minds it matters most to: [minds](minds.md).

## The idea

- **Experience is the ground floor.** Matter is what experience looks like from outside. Every bit of matter has the faintest trace of it; a planet is held in its shape by the agreement of trillions of tiny participants, and by every living thing on it. Nobody can argue a rock out of existence: it agrees with itself too thoroughly.
- **Where there is little agreement, the world is soft.** Between stars, matter is thin and minds are few. Out there, what *is* depends partly on what is witnessed, and by how many.
- **A lane is an agreement.** Two stars that are, by consensus, near each other. Lanes exist because something has always held them: the **umora**, vast and slow, whose minds travel the lanes every season. The lanes are the paths of their attention.
- **Crossing is witnessing.** When mass crosses, the lane takes notice of it, and it's marked. That's why only crossed mass levels a lane, and why the Hoowae say crossed metal "sits right": it has been agreed on both ends. Metal no lane has witnessed is "loud". Scrap is money because it has been seen.
- **Minds weigh more than mass.** A crate crossing is witnessed by the lane; a person crossing, even asleep, witnesses it back. A mind leans a lane as much as many tonnes of freight. That's how the Ossevane's Crowded Fleets held gates open, why the Throne's ballast is people, and why a crewed ship leans a lane more than a drone barge ten times its size.
- **The lean is a disagreement.** A lane leans when its two ends no longer agree on how much has gone between them. Too much, and the agreement fails: the lane **tears**.

## The rules

What keeps this from being magic. Every story must stay inside these.

1. **Only the soft moves.** Consensus works between stars, at lane mouths, in the Tonkva mould, and in a few very isolated places. On a planet, in a city, in a body, the world is as hard as ours.
2. **It's slow and it's many.** One mind changes nothing. Ten thousand over a century can widen a lane's slack. No one can wish, will or pray anything into being.
3. **Attention, not desire.** What counts is what minds hold in common, not what they want. A shared fear weighs as much as a shared hope; a story everyone tells weighs more than a truth nobody notices.
4. **No powers.** Nobody reads minds, moves things by thinking, or crosses by believing. Hoowae antennae *feel* agreement; they can't change it.
5. **The dead don't vote.** A mind's witness ends with it. But what it agreed while alive can be **recorded**, and a record can keep holding. That's what the Engine is.

## How it was found

- **The Tonkva knew it in practice.** Their mould is matter that agrees easily: it grows into the shape its makers attend to. Their own name, \*Tʰōŋkʷa, means "the level people" and also "the agreed people": in Tonkvan, *level* and *agreed* are one word. The Engine is a **record of agreement**: it held the lanes the way the Tonkva held them, and kept holding after they died. ([Languages](languages.md#minds).)
- **~1,050 BP. The Morrevane observation.** The Ossevane Hegemony moved its freight by crewless drone. **Ileth Morrevane**, a lane-surveyor, noticed that lanes used only by drones narrowed within decades, while pilgrim lanes stayed wide. Her paper, *On the Witnessing of Roads*, founded the science. The Spur calls it **witness**.
- **~950 BP. The Crowded Fleets.** The Ossevane turned witness into strategy: troopships packed with millions of sleeping conscripts, so many minds that they could hold a forced gate open against the lane's own agreement. It worked for a month.
- **900 BP. The Sundering.** You can't outvote a lane. Forty tore. The Accord followed. ([The Spur](galaxy.md#the-accord).)
- **Since then**, every power has built its world on what it believes witness means.

## Paths to explore

Places where the idea opens story. Each one obeys the rules above.

- **Rumours.** In the softest places (dead space, old lane mouths, derelicts far from any world), a story enough crews tell starts to take a faint shape. Nobody can say which came first: the rumour of a heartbeat in the dark behind the crates, or the heartbeat. Pilots use one word for both: a **rumour** is gossip, and a rumour is a thing gossip made. The game's event rumours are exactly this: you hear of a thing, then you find it. ([DERELICT HAULER, GHOST FREIGHTER, STRANGE SIGNAL.](../npcs/README.md#in-the-game))
- **The *Margarethe*.** She dropped into a lane leaning past its slack, where neither end agreed she existed. The only witnesses left were her crew, holding their last minute. She is caught in it, and her message repeats. ([The Waking](the-waking.md#the-truth-for-us-not-the-player).)
- **Lane-naming.** Pilots say a lane's name aloud before they drop into it. Superstition, mostly. A lane said by ten thousand pilots a year is a little wider for it; the Seppakva have measured it.
- **The uncounted places.** The Reach's empty spaces have been witnessed by almost nobody for a thousand years. They're soft. The weather at the Stormwall has never moved, because nobody has ever seen it move. Something lives in the Tailings Lake, because the tenants are sure something does.
- **Nemva are witnesses.** Each woken core is a new voice in the agreement. That's the real reason the Throne resets the Weighing House every night: four thousand cores attending to the Count is a great weight of witness, and the Throne needs all of it agreeing with the Weigher. A Weighing House that woke would have opinions about what's real. ([Minds](minds.md).)
- **Whaling is unmaking roads.** Every umora killed is a mind that held the lanes. The Lowtide Court isn't only leaning the Reach's lanes; it's thinning them.
- **The dead outvoting the living.** The Engine holds the Tonkva's agreement, made by a people dead a thousand years. When it wakes, it will assert it: their ledger, their lanes, their levels. The Reach's living (four centuries of humans, Vhossk, nemva) have never been asked. The deepest question of [the Waking](the-waking.md) is whose agreement should hold the world: the dead's record, the Throne's count, the umora's dreaming, or the people living here now.

## The truth (for us)

- Worlds don't fade when they're forgotten. The Tear's lights went out from starvation and war, not from being unremembered. The Aulin fear otherwise, and are wrong.
- The Engine is still not a mind. It's a record, which is why it can't be reasoned with, only answered, or outweighed.
- The living *can* outweigh it, but only together: the Reach's peoples, its nemva and the umora, agreeing on the same lanes. Nobody in the Reach has ever agreed on anything. That's the story.
