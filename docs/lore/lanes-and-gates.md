# How the lanes work

The rules of the Reach. Everything in the lore must stay inside these. Overview: [lore](README.md).

## Lanes

- A **lane** is a passage between two stars. Nobody built them: they are **agreements**, held in being by the minds that travel them, the umora first of all ([mind and matter](consensus.md)). Where one ends near a star, a ship can drop into it and come out at the other end in hours instead of centuries.
- **A lane must stay level.** Mass carried one way pulls the lane towards that end. A little lean is slack, and harmless. Too much, and the lane narrows, ships come out of it wrong, and in the end it tears: it is gone for good. No lane in the Reach has torn since the Tonkva, though the Hoowae remember three; outside, the Sundering tore forty ([the Spur](galaxy.md#the-accord)).
- **Only crossed mass levels a lane.** Sending rock back doesn't help; the lane only counts mass that has itself crossed it before. Crossing is **witnessing**: a lane takes notice of what passes through it, and only what both ends have agreed on can balance it. The Hoowae feel it: metal that has crossed "sits right", and metal that hasn't is "loud". ([Mind and matter](consensus.md).)
- **The umora** (lane-whales) swim lanes without gates, both ways, every season, and pass a locked gate as if it isn't there. A lane the umora use is always level and always open, because their minds are what hold it. A ship that keeps close in a pod's wake crosses with them and leans nothing. The [Long Herd](../factions/long-herd.md) live this way. That's why the Tonkva never hunted them, and why the Reef Pirates' whaling worries the people who know.

## Gates

- A **gate** is a Tonkva ring built on a lane's mouth. It holds the lane open for ships, and it keeps count: what went through, which way, and what's owed.
- A gate **locks** when the ledger stops: rather than let unlevel traffic tear its lane, it shuts and stays shut. This is the Tonkva's safety, and it is what caused the Hush.
- In Tonkva times every gate reported to **the Engine** on Glacis (old name *Aewhae*), which kept the whole Reach's ledger, and sent **ballast** back along any lane that leaned: barges of crossed metal, run by the Engine's **recovery units**.
- The Tonkva's beacons (the monoliths on Kessra among them) read the ledger out to passing ships, as tones. To a human instrument it sounds like a song in prime numbers.

## The Kindling: lit gates

- Humans can't unlock a gate. They **force** one (the Ostrand exiles brought the knowledge with them, and it's why they were exiled): enough reactor power poured into the ring holds the lane open anyway. A forced gate glows, so pilots call it a **lantern**. The first, in 94 L, gave the Old Lantern system its name.
- Forcing doesn't level anything. Every forced crossing leans the lane a little further, and only a Hoowae lane-reader can say how far.
- So the Syndicate, which paid for the reactors, charges a **toll** at every lantern, in crossed metal, and ships it back the other way on **tithe barges**. It keeps the lanes from tearing, roughly. It has never kept them level: the Reach has leaned outward from Halcyon for three hundred years.

## The Accord and the Throat

- Forcing is the gravest crime in the Spur, since the Ossevane Hegemony forced its own gates in a war and tore forty lanes ([the Accord](galaxy.md#the-accord)). Every lantern in the Reach is a crime under it.
- **The Throat** is the one lane from the Reach to the Spur. The Compact forced its Reach end in 241 L. The Throne holds it. Since 241 L its traffic, tribute above all, has leaned it outward: the worst lean in the Reach.

## Scrap

- **Scrap** is any metal that has crossed a lane: the wreck of a ship that came through a gate, a crate from another system, a part made off-world. Because tolls are paid in it, it's the Reach's money.
- That is why wrecks are worth fighting over, why salvagers follow battles, why the rustwings' ships are patched from other ships, and why "the tin" (your scrap) sinks off the screen after a fight: you're scooping up money.
- Metal dug and smelted on a planet that has never left it is just metal. It buys nothing at a gate.

## Wardens and the Sweep

- The Syndicate doesn't run the gates itself. It **leases** each one to whoever holds that system (the Hesk, the Compact, the Uukhvar, Vantorr...), who keeps the reactor fed and the tolls paid. The leaseholder is **the Warden**. The old pilots' word is *talter*, from the Tonkva *tāl-tōr*, "keeper of the hold".
- Two gates have no lease: the Hive's bloom has grown over Mirefall's, and Glacis's is the Engine's own. Nobody collects tolls there; something else guards them.
- **The Sweep** is the Syndicate's collection fleet. It crosses a system from the gate you came in by, seizing what's owed: ships, cargo and scrap from anyone who crossed without paying. A rustwing makes for the far gate before it arrives, and gets past the Warden however it can.

## Lattice and bloom

- The Tonkva built nothing the way we do. They **grew** their rings, towers and machines from a tame white mould that lays down ceramic and glass where it's told to. Directed, it gives **lattice**: white, hexagonal, exact. That's why lattice is white ceramic and glass.
- What told it where to grow was the ledger. With the Engine silent, the mould on the grow-moon Mirefall (old name *Morang*) kept growing with no instructions, for a thousand years. It turned violet, then it turned into the **bloom**.
- Human lattice (the Port Authority's cutters, Vantorr's drones, the old war's titans) is built round **cores** dug out of Engine City under the ice of Glacis. A core is a piece of the Engine: it still listens for the ledger. As the Engine wakes, cores twitch. And because the mould learns, a core left long enough with a crew can wake as a mind of its own ([minds](minds.md)).
