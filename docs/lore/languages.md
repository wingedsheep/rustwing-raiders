# Languages

Where the names come from. Every name in the Reach went through somebody's mouth on its way to the pilots' cant, and each mouth changed it in regular ways. This page has the old tongue, the rules each daughter language follows, and the path of every name. Overview: [lore](README.md). The peoples who speak them: [peoples](peoples.md). The voices: [`docs/alien-voices.md`](../alien-voices.md) (its sound-sets match the rules here).

**Conventions:** `*` marks a reconstructed Tonkva form (nobody has heard it spoken). `ā ē ī ō ū` are long vowels; `pʰ tʰ kʰ` are breathy stops; `kʷ` is k with rounded lips; `ŋ` is the *ng* in *sing*. `→` is "became, in that language". `L` dates count from the Landing.

## The family

```
Tonkvan (the gate-builders, dead since ~1,100 BP)
├── Hoowae ........... spoken: the Tonkva's own descendants. A thousand years of wear.
└── loanwords in:
    ├── Vhossk ........ borrowed early (~1,500–1,200 BP), in trade. Kept the old stops.
    │   └── the cant .. pilots borrowed from the Vhossk after the Kindling (94 L on)
    └── Bahwoh ........ a few words, on Halcyon (Nhal)

The ark tongue (human, rendered as English in the game)
└── the cant ......... the pilots' speech: the ark tongue plus four centuries of loans
```

The other peoples' languages (Kehx, Chikta, Eefee, Vaelo) are not related to Tonkvan; they borrowed a few ledger words, as everyone did.

**How we know Tonkvan at all:** by comparing the Hoowae words with the Vhossk loans. The Hoowae have worn the old words down to breath; the Vhossk borrowed them early and kept the hard sounds. *Hoowae* and *Tonkva* look nothing alike, but they are the same word, \*Tʰōŋkʷa, through two mouths. Hoowae scholars in the Long Quiet, and a few human ones since, worked the old tongue back out this way, and the ledger-beacons' tones confirm it.

## Tonkvan (the old tongue)

- **Sounds.** Stops in three rows: `p t k kʷ`, breathy `pʰ tʰ kʰ`; `s h`; nasals `m n ŋ`; `l r w j`. Five vowels, short and long. Syllables are simple: (C)V(C).
- **Stress** falls on the first long vowel of each part of a compound.
- **Words are built by compounding**: thing + what it does. *\*tāl-tōr*, "hold-keeper"; *\*Oru-tāl*, "seed-kept".
- **A ledger language.** Many of the roots we have are words for balance, holding and giving back, because what survives is beacon-tones and terrace-inscriptions: accounts.

## Hoowae: a thousand years of wear

The Tonkva's own descendants have spoken their tongue continuously since the Hush, and it has worn down the way spoken languages do. The changes, in order:

1. **Stops become fricatives.** `p pʰ → f`, `t tʰ → θ` (the *th* in *thin*), `k kʰ → x` (the *ch* in *loch*), `kʷ → xʷ`.
2. **Fricatives become breath.** `f θ x s → h`; `xʷ → hw`.
3. **A nasal or `l` before `h` melts into `w`.** `ŋh, ŋhw → w`; `lh → wh`.
4. **`l` and `r` soften.** Before a consonant or at the end of a word, `l r → w`; between vowels, `r` is lost.
5. **Two vowels left.** Front and low vowels (`a e i`, long or short) all become `ae`; back vowels (`o u`) become `oo`. Two vowels the same in a row merge.
6. **Endings fall.** Any final consonant but `w` is lost.

So Hoowae has **only two vowels** and hardly any consonants but `h`, `w`, `m` and `n`. It is spoken softly and slowly, on the breath, which is how Seffa sounds (`Hoo… waeha. Hoo-ae, waeha-hoo?`).

| Tonkvan | Steps | Hoowae | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| \*Tʰōŋkʷa | 1 θōŋxʷa · 2 hōŋhwa · 3 hōwa · 5 | **Hoowae** | the level people (their own name) |
| \*Āltʰa | 1–2 ālha · 3 āwha · 5 | **Aewhae** | hearth (Glacis) |
| \*Oru-tāl | 2 oru-hāl · 4 ou-hāw · 5 | **Oohaew** | the kept seed (Oru-Tal) |
| \*Tʰoŋ-tāl | 2 hoŋ-hāl · 3 howāl · 4 howāw · 5 | **Hoowaew** | the level-keeper (the Engine) |
| \*Sēpʰa | 1–2 hēha · 5 | **Haehae** | a reader, a listener |

**Learned words.** Some Tonkvan words were never worn down, because they were sacred and said carefully: the names of readers, the words of the ledger. Hoowae keeps them in their old shape beside the worn ones, the way a language keeps a scholarly word next to an everyday one from the same root. So \*Sēpʰa gives both *haehae*, any listener, and **Sefa**, a reader's name. Old Mother Seffa is *Sefa*; pilots double the *f*.

## Vhossk: the old loans

The Vhossk of Kessra traded with the Tonkva for centuries and borrowed hundreds of words. They borrowed them early, before the Hoowae wear began, so the loans keep the old hard sounds. Their own language is heavy (`gr kh v`, `uu oh`, doubled consonants), and the loans were bent to fit:

1. **`kʷ → khv`**, **`w → v`**: there is no *w* in Vhossk.
2. **Breathy stops lose their breath**: `tʰ → t`, `pʰ → p`; but `kʰ → kh`.
3. **After a long front vowel, the next consonant doubles** and the vowel shortens: `ā ē ī + C → a e i + CC`. Long back vowels stay long, written `oh` and `uu`.
4. **`ŋ` is written `ng`.**

|---|---|---|
| \*Tʰōŋkʷa | Tohngkhva | the gate-builders |
| \*Oru-tāl | Oru-tall | the kept seed |
| \*Kēs-ra | Kessra | rust-land |
| \*Wesk | Vesk | the ebb |
| \*Wan-tōr | Vantohr | share-keeper |
| \*Sāŋ-kʷen | Sangkhven | lane-ring, the junction |
| \*Mōr-āŋ | Mohrang | grow-house |
| \*Āltʰa | Allta | hearth |
| \*Tʰoŋ-tāl | Tongtall | the level-keeper |
| \*Ūmōra | Uumohra | deep-swellers (the lane-drifters) |
| \*Kʷēl-tōr | Khvell-tohr | taker-back (a recovery unit) |

The Vhossk's own words in the cant: **khesk**, a net (**Hesk**); **ghrissk**, "buzzers", their name for the winged Bahwoh (**Grisk**); **ghrohva**, "shade-carrier", their pack beast (**grova**); **Vhoss**, one of them, **Vhossk**, all of them.

## The cant: the pilots' speech

The cant is the ark tongue after four hundred years in the Reach. In the game it is written as English, so *Mirefall* and *Ashgrave* are cant names translated. Its loans came mostly from the Vhossk, in two layers, and you can tell them apart:

**The early layer** (borrowed before about 130 L):
1. **`kh → k`.** The ark tongue had no *kh*; early pilots said *k*. A *kv* at the start of a word is cut to *k*.
2. **`sk → sh`.** A sound change in the cant itself, finished by about 130 L. It caught every word borrowed before it, and none after.
3. **`oh → o`, `uu → u`, `ng` before a stop `→ n`.**
4. **Double consonants are simplified** at the end of a word and before another consonant.
5. **Unstressed final `-or` becomes `-er`.** The cant stresses the first syllable, so a final *-or* is never stressed.

**The late layer** (after about 130 L): **`kh → h`** and **`gh → g`**, and **`sk` stays `sk`**, because the change was over. Rules 3 to 5 still apply.

That's why **Vesh** and **Hesk** don't rhyme: *Vesk* was borrowed early and became *Vesh*; *khesk* was borrowed late, kept its *sk*, and lost its *kh* the late way.

| Oru-tall | early | **Oru-Tal** | 4 |
| Kessra | early | **Kessra** | (no change: the *ss* is between vowels) |
| Vesk | early | **Vesh** | 2 |
| Sangkhven | early | **Sankven** | 1, 3 |
| Mohrang | early | **Morang** | 3 |
| Allta | early | **Alta** | 4 |

| Khvell-tohr | early | **kelter** | 1 (*kv* simplified), 4, 5 |

| khesk | late | **Hesk** | *kh → h*, *sk* kept |
| ghrissk | late | **Grisk** | *gh → g*, 4 |

**Vantorr** breaks the rules on purpose. By the cant it should be *Vanter* (rule 5), and that is what pilots call the company when they're being rude. The company, founded in 250 L, had the terrace inscription read by Vhossk scholars and spelled its name their way, with a doubled *r* added for weight.

## The cant's own drift

The cant doesn't only borrow; it wears down its own words too, the way every spoken language does. Four hundred years is enough to blur the ark names:

| Ark tongue | Cant | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| First Deck | **Fersdeck** | *rst* before a consonant loses the *t*; *ir* becomes *er* |
| *Perpetua* | **Pertua** | the unstressed second syllable drops out |
| *Ansel Mor* | **Ansmor** | the unstressed *-el* drops out, and the name runs together |
| the Morang folk | **the Rang** | the stay-behinds on Mirefall stress the *last* syllable of a word (they say the Choir taught them), so the first fell away |
| Sankven | **Sankvi** | the Chikta plural *-i* replaced the *-en*: "the Sankven ones", in the half-Chikta speech of the clans |

**Power names.** Where the newer names come from:

- **Seppakva**, the order of lane-readers: Tonkvan \*Sēpʰa-kʷa, "the readers". It came into the cant early, through Vhossk *Seppakhva* (the long *ē* doubles the *p*, *pʰ → p*, *kʷ → khv*), then *kh → k*.
- **Uukhvar**, the Long Table: Vhossk *uukh*, "long", and *var*, "board". A Vhossk word, kept in Vhossk spelling because it's a name.
- **Khess**, a Hesk lodge-master: *khesk*, "net", in the Hesk lodge-speech, where *sk* at the end of a word becomes *ss*. The lodges call their master "the net".
- **Ottex Kehx**, the Ledger Clan: Kehx *ottex*, "house", and *kehx*, "the count".
- **Kelter**, **talter**: see the early-layer table above.

## Bahwoh (Halcyon)

The natives of Halcyon's twilight band speak in buzzing, rising phrases (`z b w`, `ah oh`; their wings hum under the voice). They had met Tonkva traders, but borrowed few words. One survives in the cant: Tonkvan \*ŋal, "stilt", which the Bahwoh say with a breathed *n*; the first settlers wrote it **Nhal**. The stilts of Stiltport Nhal are Tonkva pillars from an old waystation, and the town is built on them. The Bahwoh's name for their world is **Ozzawah**, "the evening that stays".

## Other tongues, briefly

- **Kehx** (the Ledger Clan's people): clipped, flat, every word ending on a click (`k t x`, `eh`). *Kehx* means "the count", *tekk* "closed, settled"; their bark *Kehx-tekk* is "the count is closed". *Ottex* is a house: the Ledger Clan is the cant's translation of **Ottex Kehx**, "house of the count".
- **Chikta** (the Skree): fast, short syllables (`t k ch`, `ee i`). *Skree* is the cant's imitation of their chatter; they call themselves **Chikta**.
- **Eefee** (Squit's people): whispered (`f s`, `ee`, `-ip`). The suffix *-ip* makes something small and dear: *Eefip*, "little Eefee", is what they call their young.
- **Vaelo** (Lady Vaelmira's people): long, languid vowels (`l m v`, `ae`). Names are strings of a family's estates: *Vael-ae-mira*.

## Minds

Two words for thinking machines, from two different kinds of borrowing ([minds](minds.md)):

- **nemva**, a woken core: Tonkvan \*nēm-wa, "one who listens" (\*nēm "listen", \*-wa "one who"). Cores were dug up after 180 L, so it's a **late** loan: Vhossk *Nemmva* (the long *ē* doubles the *m*), then the cant simplifies the double before a consonant. In Hoowae, the same word is *naemwae*.
- **Selmer**, the Throne's lane-breaker: Court Qaushen *sel-meir*, "without balance", with the cant's *ei → e* ([the end game](../endgame.md#the-selmer)).
- **Rakter**, the machine sent to Rakven: Tonkvan \*Rāk-tōr, "tear-keeper", worn down the same way as *Omter* ([the end game](../endgame.md#rakter-the-tear-keeper-rakven)).
- **Kesset**, the free minds' world: Kehx *kess-et*, "paid in full" ([the end game](../endgame.md#kesset)).
- **Tekkesh**, the Kehx closing-house on the Rim: Kehx *tekk-esh*, "where the count is closed". Written Kehx barely wears, so the cant says it as spelt ([the end game](../endgame.md#tekkesh)).
- **Ume'lua**, the Mo'ala's first world: *ume'lua*, "where the great slow ones came down". Never said to outsiders, so never worn; pilots call it the Haze ([the end game](../endgame.md#umelua)).
- **Talang**, the free nemva's meeting-place: Tonkvan \*tāl-āŋ, "house of holding", revived like *Talkwa* below, in its old shape ([the end game](../endgame.md#talang)).
- **Talkwa**, the free nemva: Tonkvan \*tāl-kʷa, "those who hold". Not borrowed at all: **revived**. The nemva read it off beacon tones and took it in its old shape, the way a people reviving a dead language picks its words from old books. It's the only Tonkvan word in use that no mouth has worn down. Pilots, hearing *talk* in it, made it **talkies**: folk etymology, as with *umora*.

## The tongues of the Spur

Languages from beyond the Throat, met in the Reach through the empire, and through older loans.

### Court Qaushen

The Throne's language of state: the speech of the Zhaun, kept formal and unchanging for two thousand years by the census, which writes everything down. Its sounds: `q` (a *k* far back in the throat), `sh zh`, `r l m n`, vowels `a i u` and the diphthongs `au ai ei`. Roots are one syllable and pile up into compounds.

| Root | Meaning | Found in |
|---|---|---|
| qau | count, number | *Qaushen*, *qaunar*, *Meirqau*, *Qaun-Ashel* |
| shen | land, the counted | *Qaushen*, *daushen* |
| meir | weight, balance | *Meirqau*, *Meirsor*, *meirdaun* |
| sor | law | *Meirsor* |
| dau | outer, sent away | *daushen*, *meirdaun* |
| sel | without, un- | *Sel-qaumai* |
| ashel | seat | *Qaun-Ashel* |

- *Qaushen*, "the counted lands": the Throne. *Meirqau*, "weigher of the count": the sovereign. *Meirsor*, "the law of weight": the Accord. *Meirdaun*, "weight sent": the Counterweight. *Daushen*, "(of the) outer lands": the viceroy of a margin. *Sel-qaumai*, "the uncounted": the Reach.

**One word, borrowed twice.** The Reach has two words for the Throne, from two meetings 900 years apart:

| When | Through | Form | |
|---|---|---|---|
| ~700 BP | the Ostrand rebels' own tongue, before the exile | *Cawshen* | Ostranders said *qau* as *caw*. The exile hymns keep it: the Compact's old word for the Throne. |
| 241 L on | the cant, as a late loan | **Koshen** | the cant has no `q` (`→ k`) and turns `au` into `o`. Pilots' word for the Throne. |

Officials write *Qaushen*. Pilots say *Koshen*. Old Compact families say *the Cawshen*, and mean it as a curse. The same pattern turns the Doshen's title *daushen* into cant **Doshen**.

### Aulin

The liturgical language of the Communion: soft, flowing, full of doubled syllables (`l n y s`, all five vowels, no hard stops). *Aulin*, "the veins"; *Ussaret*, "the keeper of the pulse"; *ninnaya*, "those who close", the Sutures; *Aunasse*, the temple-world, "where the veins meet". Aulin has no word for "road".

### Mo'ala

The Long Herd's tongue, spoken partly on the throat-sac, too low for human ears: what outsiders hear is a soft run of `m l w`, long vowels and catches of breath (`'`). *Mo'ala*, "the followers"; *Wa'aloma*, "the long herd"; *ume'ola*, "the great slow ones": the umora.

**Where *umora* really comes from.** The Tonkva didn't make the word up. They borrowed it from Mo'ala pod-riders, as *\*ūmōra*, a language with no glottal stop turning *'o* into *ō* and *l* into *r*. Then they took it apart as if it were their own (\*ūm "deep" + \*mōr "swell") and decided it meant "deep-swellers". Languages do this to loanwords all the time. So the chain is Mo'ala *ume'ola* → Tonkvan \*ūmōra → Vhossk *Uumohra* → cant *umora*.

### Kehx abroad

The Ottexa speak the same Kehx as the Ledger Clan, a thousand years apart and still understandable: the count kept them close, as written languages do. *Ottex* is "a house", *Ottexa* "the houses"; the Ottexa use it for themselves.
