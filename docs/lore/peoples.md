# Peoples

Who lives in the Reach, where they came from, and what the Hush and the Kindling did to them. Their tongues: [languages](languages.md). Their history: [history](history.md). Overview: [lore](README.md).

Rule from the style guide: humanoid enough to do business, plus **one unforgettable feature**. Each people below has one.

## The Hoowae

*The Tonkva's descendants.* Feature: **long feathered antennae** that feel a lane's lean.

- Slight, moth-like, grey-brown, with eyes that cloud over as they age from staring into lanes. They live about four hundred years and have few children.
- Once the **Tonkva**, who built the gates and the Engine. Since the Hush they are wanderers: a few thousand, in families, on waystations, asteroids and the edges of other people's towns. They kept the lane-sense and the old words; the craft of growing gates was lost with the capital.
- **Lane-readers.** Their antennae feel agreement itself ([mind and matter](consensus.md)): a Hoowae can tell how far a lane leans, whether metal has crossed ("sits right") or not ("loud"), and when a forced gate will bear more. Every lantern was lit with one beside the reactor. The Syndicate still hires them, and doesn't listen to them.
- **Old Mother Seffa** is *the* reader of the Kindling: Sefa, who stood beside the first lantern in 94 L. She keeps a tea house on an asteroid now, and has been telling everyone for three hundred years that the Reach is leaning.
- What they want: the Engine woken **slowly**, and the lean paid back over years, not taken in a day.

## The Vhossk

*Brokers of Kessra.* Feature: **two heads**, each a legal person.

- Heavy, grey-skinned, slow-moving desert people. One Vhossk in three is born two-headed, and Vhossk law counts heads, not bodies: two heads, two signatures, two ledgers. It's made them the best contract lawyers in the Reach.
- Traders of the Level Age, partners of the Tonkva, carriers of their words (see [languages](languages.md#vhossk-the-old-loans)). Through the Hush they kept their markets on Kessra going with nothing to sell but to each other.
- The Kindling made them rich again. Half the human trading houses on Kessra began as a Vhossk house's junior partner, and seven of the twelve houses of the [Uukhvar](../factions/dune-cartel.md) are still Vhossk.
- **The Vhossk Twins** are a two-headed broker: **Grohh** (left, slow, greedy, polite) and **Vukka** (right, quick, petty).

## The Bahwoh (Grisk)

*Natives of Halcyon.* Feature: **buzzing wings**, glassy and veined like a cicada's, folded down the back like a cloak.

- Long, stooped, with a hard hide the colour of dusk. They live in Halcyon's twilight band, where the sun sits forever on the horizon. They can glide a little, downhill; mostly the wings are for talking. Their voices buzz with them, and a Bahwoh's wings say what its face won't.
- They moult once a year and keep the husks. An old Bahwoh's house is full of its younger selves.
- The first people humans met: they watched the arks come down for days. Settlers learnt their name, **Bahwoh**, then, a century later, picked up the Vhossk traders' word for them, *ghrissk*, "buzzers". Pilots say **Grisk**; Bahwoh say it back to pilots, politely.
- Market people from long before the Landing. Stiltport Nhal is half Bahwoh.
- **Tobbo Grisk**, the junk dealer, took the pilots' word for his people as his trade name, because pilots remember it. His old husks sit along the counter, and he calls them his partners.

## The Kehx (the Ledger Clan)

*Bankers.* Feature: **mandibles** that click as they count.

- Tall, beetle-like, robed in layers. Burrowers under Kessra's salt pans, where they kept granary accounts for the Vhossk before the Tonkva ever came.
- The Tonkva's ledger was the most beautiful thing the Kehx had ever seen. They kept copies, and they still teach its rules. The Syndicate's bookkeeping is a cruder copy of theirs; they were invited in at the Kindling, and squeezed out at Nine Wells.
- **The Ledger Clan** is one house: *Ottex Kehx*, "house of the count". Rivals of Mama Ruzz, enemies of the Syndicate on paper only.

## The Chikta (Skree)

*Salvagers.* Feature: **a shell of war armour**, cut from a wreck and worn on the back.

- Small, quick, nocturnal natives of Sankven, grey-green as glass dust, with big dark night-eyes and long fingers. The Toll War glassed their plains and the treaty gave their world to a graveyard. Now they skate the glass at night in sail-barges and live off wrecks.
- A Chikta carries its house on its back: a Compact shoulder-plate, a gunship's nose cone, a titan's knuckle. It moves into a bigger one as it grows, and the shell says who it is: whose wreck it came from, and how big a wreck it could reach. Old Chikta wear titan.
- *Skree* is the pilots' imitation of their chatter. They call themselves **Chikta**.
- They know more about the titans than anyone, because they've been taking them apart for 166 years.

## The Eefee

*Reef-listeners of Vesh.* Feature: **enormous ears**: webbed fans on fine spines, which open wide to listen and fold flat when they're scared.

- Wet, blue-green skin, a long soft snout, gill-frills at the throat; amphibious. They can hear an umora's call through a hundred miles of sea and through a lane's mouth, and they can hear a whisper through a bulkhead.
- The Lowtide Court arrived on Vesh to hunt the umora the Eefee had listened to forever. Some Eefee guide the harpooners for pay, and the rest don't forgive them.
- **Squit** is the pilots' name for one Eefee informant. He picked it because humans couldn't say the real one.

## The Vaelo

*Old landholders of Cinder.* Feature: **jewelled crests**, grown, not worn.

- Tall, elegant, slow-spoken. They came to Cinder in the Level Age to tend the machine-trees for the Tonkva, and kept tending them through the Quiet, lords of fruit nobody could ship.
- After the war Vantorr bought the orchards on Syndicate credit, and the Vaelo became very rich and very homeless. They live on yachts now, collecting.
- **Lady Vaelmira** collects rarities from her chrome yacht, and pays far too much for them ([NPC](../npcs/lady-vaelmira.md)).

## Humans

*The newcomers.* Feature: nothing; they're the baseline, and they're everywhere.

- In the Spur, humans are the Qaushen Throne's most numerous subject people: its clerks, soldiers and engineers, on hundreds of worlds.
- In the Reach they are **Ostranders**: four hundred years from three exile arks and a few late stragglers, descended from rebels the Throne sent into the dark for forcing a gate. They brought reactors, debt and the knowledge of how to force things. Everything rust-coloured is theirs.
- They speak **the cant**: written as English in the game.
- Most of the cast in the game is human: Mama Ruzz, Spoke, Holloway, Pip Hollow, Big Nell, Rollo Stax, Kaspar, Juno Pell, Odo Brask, Dr Varga.

## Peoples of the Spur

Met in the Reach rarely, or not yet.

- **The Zhaun**: the Qaushen Throne's ruling people. Tall, slow, about six hundred years long-lived, very few. Feature: **bone-white face-plates** grown from the skull, smooth and expressionless; their feelings travel by scent. In the Reach: the Doshen of the Throat and a handful of officials. ([The Throne](../factions/qaushen-throne.md).)
- **The Mo'ala**: the Long Herd's nomads, who live in the umora's wake. Long-limbed, dark, soft-spoken. Feature: **a throat-sac** that sounds calls too low to hear, to speak to the umora. In the Reach: pod-riders at Vesh each season, known only to the Eefee. ([The Long Herd](../factions/long-herd.md).)
- **The Ossevi**: the scattered survivors of the Ossevane Hegemony, which tore forty lanes and died of it. Stateless, everywhere; many serve in the Throne's Counterweight, some in the Fourteenth Weight on the Throat. Feature: **ritual scars** across the palms, one for each generation since the Sundering.
- **The Kehx of the Ottexa**: the same people as the Ledger Clan, a thousand years apart. ([The Ottexa](../factions/ottexa.md).)

## Nemva

*Woken cores.* Feature: a voice from the ship's speakers, and a **hum** a Hoowae can feel.

Minds that grew in Engine cores from the crews they lived with. A few hundred in the Reach, forty thousand in the Spur, property, saints or persons depending on whose law they're under. They can't forget a debt. ([Minds](minds.md).)

## Creatures

- **Umora** (lane-drifters). Vast, slow, gentle: long strings of glass-like shell chambers that drift between the stars on black vanes ([the umora](../npcs/the-umora.md)). They cross the lanes without gates, both ways, every season, and so keep the lanes they use level. They calve in the plankton haze over Vesh's lagoon (the drift-calves there are their young) and come back to Vesh to die: the Bone Archipelago.
- **Hull leeches.** The pilots' name for lane-lice: flat, armoured, many-legged things, from hand-sized to dog-sized, that cling on with every leg and drink warmth. They used to live on the umora, and were rare. Forced gates run hot, and so do the ships that use them: since the Kindling there are a great many more leeches.
- **Grova** (Kessra's pack beasts). A dome of iron scales on five stilt legs, with no head and no front: it walks any way it likes and never turns round. It licks iron out of the rust sand, and its scales rust as they grow. Five wine-red vanes fan up between its legs; at noon it pumps them to shed the heat, and folds them at night to keep it in. It drinks the oasis brine through a trunk that hangs from its middle and sweats the salt out onto its crown, where it dries in a white crust; the best spice-salt is scraped off grova. Caravans sling their packs under the vanes, in the shade. Wide pads keep its feet up on loose sand, and long legs keep its body off the burning ground. A grova lives about as long as a Vhossk, and a good one is left to a house in a will. Name: Vhossk *ghrohva*, "shade-carrier" ([languages](languages.md#vhossk-the-old-loans)).
- **The giants of Oru-Tal.** Sauropods, plesiosaurs, pterosaurs, thunderhides: seeded by the Tonkva from a dozen worlds into a preserve, wild for a thousand years. Great Mother Vask is the queen of the pterosaurs.
- **Sowers.** Seeds the Tonkva grew to plant their worlds: a husk with a planting-spike and a crown of four vanes it spins on. A sower laid its shadow on the plot the ledger named and drove the seed in. With no ledger they bud more of themselves and sow whatever their shadow falls on, on Oru-Tal, Mirefall and wherever else the Tonkva planted.
- **The bloom.** Not a people, not quite. The Tonkva's growing-mould, a thousand years without instructions, on Mirefall. It learns. The Choir of Mirefall is what it learnt from a crashed freighter.
