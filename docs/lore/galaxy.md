# The Spur

The galaxy around the Reach: the great powers, the law they all live under, and the one lane that joins the Reach to them. Overview: [lore](README.md). The rules of lanes: [how the lanes work](lanes-and-gates.md). The powers' full pages: [`docs/factions/`](../factions/README.md#galactic-powers).

## Scale

- **The Spur** is the charted span of the lane-net: about **4,000 systems** along one arm of the galaxy, joined by tens of thousands of lanes. Beyond it is **the Unlit**: lanes nobody has charted, or nobody has come back from.
- **The Reach** is a cul-de-sac on the Spur's rim: about **thirty systems** in a basin of lanes, joined to the rest of the Spur by **one** lane, **the Throat**, whose Reach end is in the Meridian Reach system. The Reach is small, poor and far away. To the Spur, it matters for exactly two things: it's where the umora calve, and it's where the Engine is.
- Most of the Spur's people have never heard of the Reach, and those who have know it as a famous source of Tonkva cores at the end of the Throne's longest margin. In the Throne's ledgers it has been, for a thousand years, *Sel-qaumai*: **the Uncounted**, and the name has stuck now that it's counted.

Dozens of lesser states, kingdoms and free worlds fill the gaps between them. None of them can reach the Reach without crossing the Throne.

## The Accord

The one law every power in the Spur keeps, and the reason the Reach is a crime.

- **900 BP. The Sundering.** The **Ossevane Hegemony**, the greatest power of its age and the people who discovered that minds hold the lanes ([mind and matter](consensus.md#how-it-was-found)), lost a war against the Throne and forced its own gates, with troopships crammed with millions of minds to hold them open, faster than its lanes could bear. Forty lanes tore in a month. Two hundred systems, the Ossevane heartland among them, were cut off from the Spur for ever. That region is **the Tear**. Nobody knows what happened to the people inside; their lights went out one by one over the next century.
- **897 BP. The Accord of Level** (Court Qaushen *Meirsor*, "the law of weight"). Written by the victorious Throne, signed by every power since: **forcing a gate is the gravest crime there is.** Any power may act against a forcer, anywhere, and everyone else must help.
- The Throne wrote the Accord. The Throne has broken it in the Reach every day for 171 years, and entered the Reach's forced gates in its books as level Tonkva gates. That is the secret everything in the Reach now rests on.
- The scattered survivors of the Ossevane are the **Ossevi**: stateless, everywhere, many serving the Throne as soldiers. They remember who forced the gates, and who wrote the law afterwards.

## How each power keeps its lanes level

The lean is the Spur's great problem, and each great power is shaped by its answer. ([The rules.](lanes-and-gates.md))

- **The Throne counts.** No machine: a census bureaucracy of millions balances every lane by hand, moving goods inward and **people** outward. Conscripts, colonists, convicts and garrisons are sent to the margins as **counterweight**, to level the tribute flowing in. In the Throne a soldier is, literally, ballast.
- **The Aulin pray, and refuse.** The lanes are the veins of a living god; a lean is a sickness, a forced gate a wound. They keep their lanes level by crossing as little as possible: temple-worlds are nearly self-sufficient, and pilgrimage is rationed.
- **The Long Herd follows.** The umora swim lanes both ways and keep them level as they go. A ship that travels in a pod's wake crosses without leaning anything, and without a gate. The Mo'ala have lived this way for longer than the Throne has existed.
- **The Ottexa settles.** Lanes cross borders, so the powers' leans must be netted against each other. The Ottexa keeps the master books, and arranges the counterweight shipments that settle them. Everyone hates it; nobody can do without it.
- **The Tonkva had the Engine**, the only machine that ever did it automatically. Every power in the Spur would give a great deal to have one. The Throne has been trying to build one since it first bought Tonkva cores, fourteen centuries ago, and in earnest since the Reach's cores began flowing out through the Throat in 246 L.

## The Throat

- **Before the Hush** the Throat carried the Tonkva's trade with the Spur. The Tonkva were known outside as a small, reclusive people of a basin, who sold lattice and never visited.
- **At the Hush** the Throat locked with every other Tonkva gate. The Spur assumed the basin had died. It became the Uncounted.
- **The umora never stopped.** Lane-whales don't need gates; they pass a locked one as if it isn't there. The Long Herd has known for a thousand years that the Reach was alive, and said nothing: Mo'ala law forbids speaking of a calving ground to anyone who might go there.
- **~280 years before the Landing**, the Throne sentenced the rebels of the forge-world **Ostrand** to the **Long Dark**: three sleeper arks aimed at the Uncounted, at less than light. It was a death sentence, carried out politely. The rebels' crime was forcing a gate. (See [the Compact](../factions/halcyon-compact.md).)
- **241 L. The Opening.** Losing the Toll War, the Compact forced the Reach end of the Throat to buy outside help. The Throne's frontier fleet was waiting on the far side within a month.
- **246 L to now.** The Throne ended the war on its own terms (the Treaty of Nine Wells), chartered the [Syndicate](../factions/syndicate.md) to run the Reach for it, put a garrison and a viceroy on the Throat, and told the Spur a better story: that its lane-readers had reopened the Throat as the Tonkva built it, level, and found a basin of old Tonkva gates and core mines behind it, now the **Margin of the Uncounted**. The Spur believes it. The Throat is inside the Throne's own borders, so no one else's books cross it, and level gates are never inspected. The Throne's tribute, Engine cores above all, has flowed out through a forced gate ever since, and the lean on the Throat is the worst in the Reach.

## Time

The Spur's powers each keep their own calendars. The Throne counts **years of the Count** from the first imperial census (the Landing was in the Count's 1,512th year). The Reach counts from the Landing, **L**. This lore uses L, and BP (years before now) for older things.
