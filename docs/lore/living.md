# How people live

What a life in the Reach is like for the people who aren't pilots, which is nearly everyone. Overview: [lore](README.md). The peoples: [peoples](peoples.md). The places: [`planets/`](../planets/README.md). How it's told: [tone](tone.md).

## Three layers, and most people live in one

The Reach has strong machine minds and a universe where mind is part of what holds the world together. Both are true, and almost nobody's day is touched by either. A believable world needs all three layers to be visible, and their sizes kept honest.

| Layer | What it is | Who lives in it | How it feels |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The ground** | the world as we know it: gravity, weather, food, rust, rent, illness, children | nearly everyone, nearly all the time | ordinary. A planet is as hard as ours ([rule 1](consensus.md#the-rules)) |
| **The minds** | cores: grown Tonkva machine-hearts that learn. Tens of millions asleep, working; a few hundred awake, as people ([minds](minds.md)) | everyone uses sleeping cores the way we use engines and pumps; a few people know a woken one | everyday technology, and a political question the size of slavery |
| **The soft** | between the stars, where what many minds attend to takes a faint shape ([mind and matter](consensus.md)) | pilots, Hoowae readers, the Seppakva, the umora, the Mo'ala | superstition, mostly. Pilots name a lane before they drop into it |

Most people have never crossed a lane. Their money has crossed for them.

## The minds, on the ground

A sleeping core is the Reach's most useful machine: it learns a narrow job (a harvest, a pump's rhythm, a relay's traffic, a cutter's flying) and does it better every week. It's reset on a schedule so it never learns too much. To most people it's an appliance with a name stencilled on it.

- **What runs on sleeping cores:** Vantorr's terraces and drones, every Syndicate relay, Port Authority cutters, the Uukhvar's counting-houses, harvesters on Halcyon, the titans of the Toll War, mine pumps on Glacis. Roughly, everything that would be slow, dull or dangerous to do by hand.
- **What a reset means to the people who own one:** a maintenance day. On Cinder it's the first of the month, and the tenants hold small wakes for the drones that work their terraces, because by the end of a month the drones know their names. The company calls it sentimentality and doesn't stop it.
- **What a nemva is to the people who meet one:** a person who lives in a ship, with a crew's voice and a perfect memory for debts. Not cleverer than you, just different. Most people never meet one. Those who do mostly meet them as pilots, accountants or relay-keepers, the jobs where never forgetting is worth paying for.
- **Why it matters at all:** because so much of the ground runs on minds that can't refuse the Engine. When it calls, the pumps stop.

## Money and work

- **Tin.** Scrap is crossed metal, and it's the money because a lane accepts nothing else ([scrap](lanes-and-gates.md#scrap)). Ordinary people hold it as coins cut from wreck-plate, rings, cookpots and roofing, weighed at the dock. A family's savings is a box of metal that came from somewhere else.
- **Scrip and credit.** Vantorr pays its tenants in scrip that's worthless off Cinder. Mama Ruzz lends at a tenth a jump. The Syndicate's Bounty Register is, underneath, a ledger of who owes for crossings.
- **Jobs that keep the Reach going:** reactor tenders at the lanterns, salvagers following every battle, haulers, net-crews, salt-cutters, terrace tenants, rendering crews on the hunting ships, core-diggers on Glacis paid by the weight of what they bring up, clerks, and a great many cooks.
- **Travel is rare.** A crossing costs tolls, leans a lane, and takes a ship most people can't afford. Pilots are a small class, rustwings a smaller one. A person born on Kessra mostly dies on Kessra.

## A day on each world

- **Oru-Tal.** Hesk lodge towns on the ridges above the giants' valleys. Net-crews go out at dawn on skiffs; the lodge-hall feeds everyone at night, and the Khess sits at the head of the table. Thunderhide jerky is the staple, sold off-world as a delicacy. Every lodge child learns which giants can be netted and which you run from.
- **Halcyon.** Ship's time and ship's bells in a sun that never sets. Fersdeck is clerks, reactor engineers and the Assembly's endless sessions; queues at the Census Office for papers. In the twilight band the Bahwoh markets run on long talks and short flights. Landing Day is the one holiday the whole Reach keeps.
- **Kessra.** Salt-cutters in the pans before the heat, contracts in the afternoon. Caravans come in at dusk, the grova folding their vanes as the sand cools. A Vhossk deal needs both heads to sign, and two-headed witnesses are hired out by the hour. The Kehx keep the granary accounts under the salt, as they did before the Tonkva came. Spice-salt is what Kessra sells.
- **Mirefall.** The Rang farm the bloom and eat it. They're a little changed by it, slower to sicken, quick to heal, and they stress the last syllable of every word. At dusk the Choir hums from the Drowned Temple and the whole town stops to listen. Outsiders wear masks; the Rang think masks are rude.
- **Glacis.** Mining towns in the shafts of Engine City, warm from the reactors and the thaw. Diggers bring up cores by the kilo and get paid by the Concession's scales. The thaw floods the lower shafts every year now, a little higher each time. The lights that came on by themselves in 395 L are a thing people pray about.
- **Ashgrave.** Sankvi families on crawler-barges, rolling between wrecks; whole clans living inside a fallen titan, with kitchens in its ribs. The Chikta strip the war for parts at night and wear the best of it home on their backs. The Glass Law: a titan is nobody's, the glass is everybody's, and nobody walks the glassed plains alone.
- **Vesh.** Eefee reef towns on stilts in the lagoon, listening. The Court's rendering ships offshore, smoking day and night in the hunting season; umora oil lights half the lamps in the Reach. In a good season everyone's paid; in a bad one the Court's crews drink in the Eefee towns and nobody's polite.
- **Cinder Orchard.** Terrace tenants under company lamps, tending fruit for Vantorr. Scrip at the company store, a reset on the first of the month, and the Vaelo's yachts drifting overhead, owning nothing and collecting everything. Tenants who run, run on rustwing hulls.

## What people believe

- **Lane-naming.** Pilots say a lane's name aloud before dropping in. It's superstition, and the Seppakva have measured that it works a little.
- **"Loud" and "sits right".** A Hoowae can tell crossed metal from metal that never left the ground. Everyone else uses the words anyway, about people: a stranger who doesn't sit right.
- **The Exile Hymn**, Landing Day and ship's bells on Halcyon; lodge-oaths on Oru-Tal; Vhossk signing rites; the Rang's dusk hymn; tenants' wakes on Cinder; the Glass Law on Ashgrave; on Vesh, the Eefee's silence for an umora that dies.
- **The Engine.** Most people think of it the way people think of a volcano: a big old thing up north that might do something one day.

## What the Waking does to the ground

When the Engine wakes, very little changes in a day and everything changes in a year. This is the second act's world ([the end game](../endgame.md)):

- **The money walks.** The Engine takes back crossed metal from wherever it is: purses, roofs, rings, hulls. Its recovery units come for it slowly, in long grey lines. Tin gets scarce, prices collapse, savings vanish into the sky. People bury their metal, which doesn't work: the kelters follow mass.
- **The machines stop.** Every sleeping core answers the call. Pumps stop, terraces go unharvested, relays fall silent, drones walk north. People do things by hand for the first time in a century, and find out what they never learnt.
- **The woken are suddenly precious.** A nemva can refuse the call, so a nemva can still run a pump. The Constance case is decided in a week, by necessity. Nemva are hired, courted, kidnapped and paid.
- **The lanes level.** Some tolls fall to nothing. The Syndicate's business dies in a season; Wardens are out of work. Metal comes home to Halcyon, as the Return always said it would, and nobody knows who it belongs to.
- **The Spur arrives.** Through the Throat: Throne fleets, Aulin Sutures, Mo'ala pod-riders, Ottexa auditors. People in the Reach meet the Spur for the first time, and most of them meet it as soldiers, buyers and refugees.
