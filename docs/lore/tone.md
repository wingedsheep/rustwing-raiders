# Tone

How the Reach should feel, and how a place, a person or a boss is told. The look and the cast: [style and characters](../style-and-characters.md). Overview: [lore](README.md).

## The vibe

**Many ways of holding the world together, meeting in one small place.** The Spur isn't an empire and its rebels. It's several whole ways of being a civilisation, each grown from its own answer to the one problem everyone has, the lean ([the Spur](galaxy.md#how-each-power-keeps-its-lanes-level)), and each sure its answer is what keeps the lanes from tearing:

| Order | Holds the world by | What it can't admit | Whom you might meet |
|---|---|---|---|
| [**The Throne**](../factions/qaushen-throne.md) | counting: a census of everything; nobody starves, and the lanes are levelled with people sent away as ballast | its Count, the thing that holds it real, has a lie in it (the Reach); and its government runs on minds it wipes nightly, which once woke and counted the Ballast by name | the blind Weigher and the human clerk who guides his pen; the Doshen who has come to love the Reach; an Ossevi captain guarding a forced gate |
| [**The Aulin**](../factions/aulin.md) | refusing: crossing as rarely as they can, keeping a lamp for every lost system | if their god is all minds agreeing, a new kind of mind could change it, so they lock up their newest minds; and their mercy to the lanes starves the people who live by them | a Pulse who was a Suture; a Suture in the Reach who hasn't sent her report; an oracle that has never seen the veins |
| [**The Long Herd**](../factions/long-herd.md) | following: crossing only in the umora's wake, leaning nothing | their way works only for those who give up everything, and their law of silence let the Tonkva die alone and lets the whaling go on | a Herd-Speaker whose herd lost its bull to Istra; a pod-rider who already bends the law |
| [**The Ottexa**](../factions/ottexa.md) | settling: every power's books netted against every other's | never lying isn't telling the truth: it has kept the Spur's worst secrets, and its honesty is a weapon it chooses when to fire | the deciding vote, reading her house's unsent letters; the oldest free mind in the Spur; a nervous satchel-clerk |
| [**The Tonkva**](tonkva.md) (dead) | a machine that agreed for them | they made fairness automatic and forgot how to do it themselves; their own Lock shut their outposts in | only in records: the one who wrote the Lock, the one who grew the Engine, the reader nobody believed |
| [**The nemva**](minds.md#a-people-arriving) (arriving) | nobody knows yet | each is made of a crew and a ledger, and the Engine's call is the nearest thing they have to a parent | *Patience*, *Constance*, *Unbending*; Tekkat; the Night Clerk, born every night and wiped every morning |

Each began as a kindness (the Throne's to the hungry, the Aulin's to the lost, the Herd's to the umora, the Ottexa's to anyone who needed a fair count, the Tonkva's to the cold), and each has a thing at its heart it can't admit. Each is humane by its own lights and monstrous by another's: to the Throne a reset is maintenance; to the Ottexa it's murder; to the Aulin a nemva crossing a lane is sacrilege. None of them is the villain, and none is one voice: each has its factions, and its people who don't fit. They grind against each other at the edges, slowly, the way continents do.

**Something larger.** Under the politics is the question the lore keeps coming back to ([mind and matter](consensus.md)): the world is held firm by minds agreeing, so *whose agreement holds?* The Throne's count, the Aulin's god, the umora's dreaming, a dead people's ledger, or the new minds, who have never agreed on anything? Each order is, underneath, a bid to be what reality agrees with. In the Reach, a backwater none of them was watching, the dead people's answer is waking up, and it doesn't negotiate. For every order in the Spur that is a thing from outside its thinking: nobody has a plan for it, though everybody thinks they do.

- **You are small, and it's mostly over your head.** The great orders are vast, slow and not looking at the Reach. The player sees their edges: a census officer at a dock, a disguised knight, a pod-rider, a clerk writing letters, a beacon's tones. Treat the scale with a dry, humane wit, not awe.
- **Rustwings live in the cracks.** They belong to no order. The Reach is the one place where the orders don't agree, and the gap between them is where a patched ship can fly. They aren't rebels: they're the noise between systems, and they like it there.
- **Everyone's caught between two orders.** The Compact between the Throne that exiled it and the navy it hides; the Table between its own contracts and a dead people's ledger; the Court between its hunt and the Herd it doesn't know exists. That grinding is where the stories are.
- **Minds are people, with names and opinions.** A woken ship is a character, not a tool: *Patience*, *Constance*, *Unbending*. How an order treats its minds is how the story judges it, without ever saying so.
- **Charm under pressure.** Rustwings talk their way through as often as they shoot. Bars, banter, a bad deal taken with a grin.

## How it's told

The Reach tells its story the way a ruin does: a piece at a time, in the voice of whoever is in it. Our rules for that:

1. **Everyone owes, and the debt is who they are.** The lore's one idea is a debt that's physical, so every character is defined by what they owe and to whom. A boss doesn't guard a gate because it's the boss: it's paying something off, keeping a vow, or collecting what it's owed. Write that first.
2. **The call.** One pull runs through the whole Reach: the Engine's tone, read out by every beacon, felt by every core. Everyone answers it in their own way, and the answer is what changes them. Each boss's last phase shows its answer.
3. **Few words, said once, by the one who means them.** No narrator, no exposition. A boss gets a line as it comes, a voice under its bar while it fights, and a line as it goes. A place gets a one-line card. A beacon gets its tones. That's all.
4. **One sad truth under each.** Every boss and every recurring character has one fact that turns them from an obstacle into someone: the player can learn it (by how they win, in a later event, from a dock rumour), and nobody ever explains it.
5. **Grandeur, broken recently.** The Tonkva were a thousand years ago, not a million. Their things are beautiful, half working, and still doing their jobs for nobody. People live in the bones of bigger things: a whale, a titan, an ark, a temple.
6. **Travellers on the same road.** Some of the cast are going somewhere (to Glacis, out through the Throat, home), and you meet them again further along: older, poorer, closer. Their stories end, sometimes well.
7. **The ship's log.** What the player knows about a foe, a place or a person is written by **their own ship's core**, in the crew's voice: short, wry, sometimes wrong. As the core learns the crew ([combat and builds](../combat-and-builds.md)), the log gets more personal. (An idea, not built.)
