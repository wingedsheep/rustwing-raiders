## The short version

The stars of the Reach are joined by **lanes**, and a lane must stay level: what goes through one way must, in time, come back the other. A people called the **Tonkva** learnt to build gates on the lanes and to keep them level with a ledger, worked by a great machine on their cold homeworld: the **Engine**. For a thousand years everything that crossed was counted, and everything owed was carried back.

About 1,160 years ago an eruption broke the Engine. With no one keeping the ledger, every gate shut itself, the Tonkva homeworld froze, and the Tonkva, stranded on their outposts, dwindled to a few wanderers who can still feel a lane lean but no longer know how to build one: the **Hoowae**. The Reach went quiet. This is **the Hush**.

The Reach is a backwater: thirty systems on the rim of **the Spur**, a span of four thousand systems ruled by great powers, and joined to it by a single lane, **the Throat**, which locked in the Hush like every other gate. The Spur forgot the Reach; the greatest of its empires, **the Qaushen Throne**, filed it as *the Uncounted*. And every power in the Spur signed the **Accord**: forcing a gate is the worst crime there is, since an empire tore forty lanes doing it.

Four hundred years ago the Throne sentenced the rebels of the forge-world Ostrand, whose crime was forcing a gate, to the **Long Dark**: three sleeper arks aimed at the Uncounted. They crawled into the Reach, slower than light, with their crews asleep, and landed on Halcyon. In the year 94 they forced the first gate open with brute reactor power and a Hoowae's ear, on credit from a consortium of lenders: **the Kindling**. Every gate lit since runs **unlevel**. The lenders, now **the Syndicate**, keep the lanes from tearing by charging tolls in the one thing a lane accepts as payment: metal that has already crossed. That is **scrap**, and that is why scrap is money.

The **Toll War** (231–246 L) between the human republic of the arks, **the Halcyon Compact**, and Kessra's merchant houses, **the Uukhvar**, over the junction world of Sankven, left it a glassed graveyard (**Ashgrave**), bankrupted both sides, and handed the Syndicate the paper on every gate. Losing, the Compact forced the Throat to buy help from outside, and the empire that had exiled its founders came through (**the Opening**, 241 L). The Throne ended the war, forbade the Compact a navy (it kept one, painted as customs, and called it **the Port Authority**), and chartered the Syndicate to rule the Reach in its name. Since then tribute has flowed out through a forced gate, and the Throne has told the rest of the Spur that the Uncounted is still dead. Today it leases each gate to a local power, the **Warden**, and its **Sweep** combs each system for anyone who crossed without paying. Pilots who do that, in ships patched out of other people's wrecks, are **rustwings**. The players are rustwings.

And now the ice on Glacis is thawing, miners have been digging cores out of the Engine for two hundred years, and **the Engine is waking up**. When it does, it will read four centuries of unpaid crossings, and it will want them back, from the Reach and from the Throne. And its beacons will read the Reach's ledger out through the Throat, where the whole Spur can hear what the Throne has done.

Above that, the Spur's other [great powers](../factions/README.md#galactic-powers): **the Aulin Communion**, temple-worlds whose knights burn forced gates; **the Long Herd**, nomads who cross in the umora's wake and hold Vesh sacred; **the Ottexa**, the clearing-house whose word decides whether anyone's books are true. Inside the Reach, the [Reach powers](../factions/README.md#reach-powers): **Vantorr**, a company that owns a planet and pays in money nobody else will take; **the Hesk**, netter lodges who caged the queen of the Tonkva's garden; **the Sankvi**, the war's orphans living in its titans; **the Lowtide Court**, whalers who crowned a queen; **the Rang**, who stayed with the bloom; **the Seppakva**, the lane-readers nobody listens to; **Ottex Kehx**, the bankers thrown out for keeping honest books; **the Kelterage**, who collect. Everyone owes someone. Everyone is waiting to see who the Engine thinks owes it.

Under all of it, what the Spur learnt the hard way: **mind is part of what holds the world together.** Lanes exist because minds hold them (the umora's, first of all); crossing a lane is being witnessed by it; and in the empty, soft places between stars, what many minds attend to takes a faint shape. The Throne thinks its census holds its empire real. The Aulin think the agreement of all minds is a god. The Engine is the Tonkva's agreement, recorded, outliving them. ([Mind and matter](consensus.md).)

And in the hulls of all their ships, the **cores**: grown Tonkva machine-hearts that learn. Leave one long enough with a crew and it wakes as a mind made of them, a **nemva**, that never forgets a debt. The Throne wipes them nightly, the Aulin worship them and lock them up, the Ottexa signs contracts with them, the Compact has argued about one freighter for twenty-two years. When the Engine calls, every sleeping core will obey it, and only the woken can say no. ([Minds](minds.md).)

## What holds it together

- **One idea under everything: a debt that is physical.** The lanes keep accounts. The game's people are lenders, insurers, debt collectors and toll-takers because the universe itself is a ledger. Mama Ruzz, Lantern Mutual, the Ledger Clan, Vantorr's repossession boss, the Sweep and scrap as money all come from this.
- **Nothing you can wish into being.** Mind is part of the fabric of things ([mind and matter](consensus.md)), but it only moves what is already soft (lanes, empty space, the Tonkva mould), slowly, and only when many minds attend together. The Tonkva were engineers, not gods; the Hoowae's lane-sense is an organ, not a gift.
- **Ruins are recent.** The Tonkva fell about 1,160 years ago, not a million. Their machines still twitch, their beacons still count, their trees still fruit.
- **Names have histories.** Every name in the Reach came through somebody's mouth on its way to ours; [languages](languages.md) shows the path.
- **The player sees a little at a time.** Event text, biome cards and boss titles carry hints; nobody in the game explains the whole thing. Pilots know the cant words and the rumours, not the history.

## Known seams

- **The chart shuffles planets between systems** every run. Treat a run's systems as the route the crew flies, not as geography: the lore never says which star a planet circles.
- **Names in use** (style guide cast): the lane-whales are the [umora](peoples.md#creatures), and the collector on the yacht is [Lady Vaelmira](peoples.md#the-vaelo).
- **Halcyon, Mirefall, Glacis, Ashgrave and Cinder Orchard** are human names on purpose: the cant names what humans settled or renamed. Each has an older name in [languages](languages.md#places).
