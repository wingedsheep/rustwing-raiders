# The simple run

The plan for the simplification push. Rustwing at its best is four things: **flying stages, picking up parts, making a build, and fighting Wardens.** Everything else in a run either feeds those four or goes. Drafted 2026-10-25.

## Why

The game has grown many layers that are each good on their own, and too many together:

- **The run**: fuel, the Sweep, debt and interest, insurance, bounties, reputation, favours, passengers, contracts, markets, cargo, 33 events and 8 minigames.
- **The missions**: raid, hunt, escort, heist and boarding (a whole on-foot game, ~8k lines).
- **The build**: frames, the grid, hardpoints, links, load, edges, marks, arsenal states, tiers, mods, affixes, traits, kit, grafts and bonds, and the core's veins, knots, knacks, talismans, imprints, room, leaning, agreements, waking and the Engine's call.
- **Staying alive**: hull, a regenerating shield with refills, the Weld, repair pickups, +20 hull a level, docks, dealers, and affixes that heal.

A player meets about a dozen build systems before their first Warden. Most of them are percentages you can't feel at speed. The best games in the genre do much less, and do it very well.

## What the best do

### ZeroRanger: few weapons, every stage its own idea
- **A choice of two weapons after a stage**, and that's the build. Every pick changes how you play, so it never feels like a stat.
- **Each stage has its own trick** and a mini-boss halfway, so stages are easy to tell apart and remember.
- **Hard but never unfair.** You always have the tools to survive, and the controls never let you down.
- **Not for us:** the narrow screen and the bullet-hell peaks. Rustwing stays wide, and danger comes from fewer, readable things, not walls of bullets.

### Sky Force Reloaded: short stages, visible growth, reasons to fly again
- **Short stages (a few minutes) with a big boss**, made of destructible parts.
- **Power you can see.** An upgraded cannon fires more and bigger shots, so you feel each purchase on screen.
- **Medals per stage** (kill 70%, kill 100%, no damage, rescue everyone) give a reason to fly a stage again, and replaying teaches it.
- **Not for us:** the grind for stars and the look. Rustwing already looks better; we take the pacing and the visible growth.

### Hades: offers of three, families that combine
- **Each reward is one of three**, from a "family" (a god), and changes one verb: attack, special, cast, dash. A new one on a taken verb replaces the old one.
- **You see the reward on the door before you go through it.** Picking a route is picking your build.
- **Two families together unlock a duo**: the jackpot, rare, and the moment a run clicks.
- **Poms** raise the level of something you already have, so a good early pick keeps getting better.
- **Heat** (the Pact): after a win, you choose your own difficulty one notch at a time.

Rustwing already has the Hades shape and hasn't seen it: **grafts are boons.** Grafts come from factions (the gods), take one verb each, replace on a taken verb, and three from one faction wake a bond. This push makes grafts the second half of the build.

### Dead Cells: few slots, scaling you can feel
- **Two weapons and two skills.** That's the whole loadout. Variety comes from how weapons play, not how many slots there are.
- **Three colours** (Brutality, Tactics, Survival). Scrolls raise a colour, and every item of that colour gets stronger. Picking scrolls is a simple choice with a big effect.
- **Enemies scale by biome, not by your build.** A lucky build shreds, and that's the fun.
- **Boss Cells** are difficulty steps you unlock by winning.

### Silksong: the boss is a dance you learn
- **A handful of moves**, each with one tell (pose and sound), one speed and one clean answer.
- **The boss picks its move first, then moves into position**, so it's harder to read than one that reacts to where you are, but still fair.
- **Phases change how it fights**: the Cogwork Dancers start in sync, speed up, then fall out of step.
- **Punish windows**: big moves leave it open. Patience beats greed.

The [Warden rules](bosses/README.md#what-a-finished-boss-has) already ask for this. What's missing is the time to finish them, which this push frees up.

## The run, simplified

**Three sectors. In each one, five stages and a Warden.** About 35–45 minutes for a full run.

### The chart
The orbiting solar system stays: planets go round the star, and what's in reach changes as they move. Too many games use the Slay the Spire map; ours is its own. It just loses everything that makes it a budget.

- **No fuel and no Sweep.** A sector is **five stops, then the Warden's gate.** Each jump moves the clock, so the planets turn and the next reach is different. Which site you take now decides what you can reach next. That's the whole puzzle.
- **Every site shows its reward before you go** (a part's button, a graft's faction, a refit, scrap, a repair), the way Hades shows rewards on its doors.
- **Three kinds of site:**
  - **Stage**: a plain flying stage.
  - **Elite stage**: marked on the chart. It's harder, with a set-piece elite, and pays better.
  - **Dealer**: the only shop.
- **No events, escorts or heists, and no danger pips, markets or intel.** You fly, you choose, you fly.

### The rewards (pick one of three)

| Reward | What you get | Like |
|---|---|---|
| **PART** | One of three parts, for the button the site showed. | Dead Cells weapons |
| **GRAFT** | One of three grafts from the faction you just fought. One per verb; three from a faction wake its bond. | Hades boons |
| **REFIT** | +1 level on one of three parts you carry. | Hades Poms |
| **SCRAP** | Money for the dealer. | |
| **REPAIR** | Hull back. | |

### Good rewards anywhere, better from elites and Wardens
Like Slay the Spire: elites and Wardens pay best, but any stage can turn up something good.

| Where | Each card in a PART offer | Its level |
|---|---|---|
| **Stage** | 60% common, 30% rare, 9% epic, 1% legendary | the sector's, and 15% one higher |
| **Elite stage** | at least one rare or better; 40% rare, 40% epic, 5% legendary | one higher than the sector's |
| **Warden** | its signature graft, and a pick of epic or legendary parts | two higher than the sector's |

- **Bad luck builds up**: every offer without an epic or better makes the next one a bit likelier, the way Slay the Spire raises its rare-card odds until one turns up.
- **A Warden** also gives a big repair.
- **Cross grafts** (a Hades duo): holding grafts from two factions can unlock a graft that mixes both. They're rare, and they come after the base works.

### The dealer
Three parts, a refit, a repair, a reroll, and selling. Scrap is the only money. The dealer is a cast member (a different one per sector) with one or two lines: that's where the NPCs live now.

### Staying alive
- **One hull bar.** It carries over between stages.
- **The Weld** is the skill heal (~40 s of good fighting fills it).
- **REPAIR nodes, the dealer, and a Warden kill** bring hull back. That's every source.
- **The shield goes.** Two kinds of health and refills are one layer too many; hull and the Weld do the job. Playtest it: if stages feel brutal without it, tune the hull, not a new layer.

## The build, simplified

Two halves: **parts** (what your buttons do) and **grafts** (how your verbs change).

### Parts: loot the way Dead Cells does it
In Dead Cells, every drop is a real choice: **keep what you have and make it stronger, or swap it for the new drop.** A favourite can last the whole run, and a lucky drop can change it. Rustwing's parts work the same way.

- **Every part has a level.** It comes from where it dropped: the sector sets it, and elites and Wardens add to it (see the table above). Level is the part's main power: damage, and on guns, how many barrels and how big the shots are. You can see each level on the ship, the way Sky Force shows its upgrades.
- **Every part has a rarity**, which says how much it carries on top:
  - common: nothing
  - rare: an affix
  - epic: two affixes
  - legendary: an affix and a trait
- **Affixes are rules, not percentages**: "on a perfect, fires a volley", "kills leave a BURN pool". There are about eight, and each one changes how you fly. The flat-percentage mods go.
- **Keep or replace.**
  - **Keep**: a REFIT, or the dealer, raises a part you love by a level, so it keeps up.
  - **Replace**: a new drop comes at the level where you found it, often higher than what you carry, maybe with a better affix. Its card shows what changes against the part it would replace, in green and red.
  - A replaced part is sold for scrap on the spot.
- **The marks and reactions stay** (BURN, SHOCK, CRACK), and legendary traits stay the jackpot.

### Parts on the ship: kept, made clearer
Putting parts on your ship is one of the things Rustwing has that the others don't. It stays, with fewer rules and more feedback.

- **Three rules, and only three:**
  1. Each button has its hardpoints (trigger, heavy, aux, special).
  2. Guns fire the way they sit.
  3. Boosters help what they touch.
- **Go:** links, load classes, edges, arsenal states (CHILL becomes a mark if it's worth keeping), and the kit as a separate layer. Its good pieces become parts.
- **A new part goes on in one press**: it lands where it does most, swapping with the part it beats. The hangar is for tinkering, never a chore after every stage.
- **See it before you commit.** While you place a part, the ship fires in the hangar, and a booster's light runs to what it touches. A gun's arrow shows where it will shoot.
- **Every part shows on the ship in flight**, and grows with its level.
- **The arsenal's first slice** (Frost Sprayer, Charge Cannon, Saw Launcher) lands as plain parts with levels. Gun rolls aren't needed once guns have levels.
- **About 30 parts** in the pool for Act I, each with its own personality ([parts variety](parts-variety.md)).

### Grafts
- **Keep:** one graft per verb, factions, bonds, and Warden signatures.
- **Where they come from:** GRAFT nodes (from the faction you just fought) and Wardens. An elite taken down makes the next graft offer rare. Grafts no longer need a wreck screen of their own; the offer comes with the stage's results.

### The core
The core stays as the fiction: **the core copies what it beats.** That's what a graft is. The veins, knots, knacks, talismans, imprints, room, leaning, agreements, waking and the Engine's call go. The best knacks (Cold Blood, Follow Through, Hot Rivets) come back as grafts or legendary traits.

### In the fight
- **Keep:** fire, heavy, dash with perfects, the special, boost with Burn, the Weld, and Plating → Open → Takedown (it makes elites a fight, not a sponge).
- **The aux is a frame's thing:** GUARD frames have the parry, and others get an aux from parts.
- **Check the verbs on the recorder** (below): a button nobody presses goes.

## Stages that play well

### A stage has a shape
A stage lasts about 2½–3 minutes and is built from beats, in this order:

1. **Intro**: one formation that shows the stage's foe.
2. **Build-up**: two or three formations that mix it with others.
3. **Set piece**: the mid-stage elite, or a terrain moment (a canyon run, a span to break, guns dug into a cliff).
4. **Breather**: ground targets to strafe, pickups, the biome's scripted moment.
5. **Peak**: overlapping formations, the hardest part.
6. **Out**: the last wave, then the results.

### Built from wave cards
The director builds stages from **wave cards** instead of free procedural waves:

- **A card is a hand-made formation** in the wave lab's `ScriptWave` format. It holds a faction, its foes, how they come in, what it tests (dodge, priority, crossfire, terrain), and what it costs in threat.
- **Each beat has a threat budget** and draws cards to fill it. The budget grows by sector.
- **Sectors unlock harder cards.** Later sectors get new combinations and tighter timing, not more bullets.
- **Each faction flies its own cards**, so how they come at you says who they are.
- **Many weak foes, few heavies.** A beat is filled with fodder and mediums first; heavies and elites are rare and spaced out, never back to back. Even the first stage keeps you busy. (The first playtest: stage 1 was too sparse, and stage 2's run of high-HP ships was the wrong kind of hard.)
- **Each stage has one idea** (ZeroRanger): a biome trick that stays for the whole stage. For example: crosswind, a dark stage lit by searchlights, a minefield lane, or a worm chasing from below.

### No bullet hell
- **A cap on enemy bullets on screen**, per sector (roughly 40, then 60, then 80; tune on the recorder).
- **Fewer, readable bullets.** Danger comes from aimed shots, lanes, timing and combinations.
- **Medals** (Sky Force), later: three small goals per stage, shown on the node (kill 90%, no hit, take down the elite). Each pays scrap. They give a reason to fly well without adding a system.

## Wardens that are fun

Every Warden gets a Silksong pass, against [what a finished boss has](bosses/README.md#what-a-finished-boss-has):

- **Four to six moves**, each with one tell (pose and sound, 0.6–1 s of wind-up), one speed and one clean answer.
- **It picks a move, then moves into position.**
- **No filler bullet patterns** between set pieces. Its idle is a move, too.
- **Phase 1** plays the moves one at a time, to be learnt. **Phase 2** strings them together and adds one or two. **Phase 3** changes the arena or the rules.
- **Every big move leaves it open**: a window for a heavy or a takedown.
- **About 90–150 s** at the expected power for its sector. It should take three to five tries to learn.
- **A Warden rematch** on the title screen once you've met it: for practice, and for playtests.
- **Mid-bosses**: each planet's set-piece elite is a small dance of two or three moves, using the same rules.

Each Warden is still taken to a full score one at a time, flown by hand before the next.

## Scaling with the ship's power

Enemies scale by **where you are**, never by how strong your build is (like Dead Cells and Hades). A lucky build shreds, and that's the fun. Skill and a good route make up for bad luck.

### Expected power
- **Model the expected build at each stage**: the bench's ladder of ships, rebuilt by letting the autopilot pick at random from real reward offers, stage after stage, over many seeds.
- **Measure its damage per second** against each enemy class.

### Time to kill, the target
Set enemy hull and Warden HP so that the **median build** hits these times:

| Enemy | Time to kill |
|---|---|
| Fodder | under 0.3 s |
| Medium | 1–2 s |
| Plated (to Open) | 2–3 s |
| Elite | 6–10 s |
| Warden phase | 30–50 s |

A top-10% build should take about half that. A bottom-10% build should take no more than double; if it does, the dealer always offers a trigger gun.

### Danger grows by composition
- **Enemy damage barely scales.** Later sectors hurt more because foes come in harder combinations and tighter timing, not because each bullet hits for more or the screen fills up.
- **Pressure**, later: after a win, choose your difficulty a notch at a time (like Hades' Heat). Each notch is one plain rule: elites in every stage, faster bullets, no REPAIR nodes.

### Hull targets, the check
For the median player, about **25–40% of the hull lost per stage before healing**, with Wardens costing more. Deaths should spread over the run, not pile up on one stage or Warden. The recorder checks this.

## You play, I tune: the flight recorder

This is the most important part of the push. The loop: **I ship a playtest build with notes on what changed. You play it. I read the logs and replays, and tune. Then the next build.**

### One run at a time
We polish **one run until it feels absolutely perfect**, and only then go on to the next.

- **A run is a fixed seed.** The same chart, the same stages, the same Warden and the same reward offers every time you fly it. A change then shows as a change, not as luck, and your logs from one build compare with the next.
- **"Perfect" is your call**, backed by the logs:
  - every stage and the Warden rated fun
  - no "unfair"
  - hull lost and times to kill inside the targets
  - deaths only where you agree you made the mistake
- **Every build says what changed and what to watch**, in a short note: "the second stage's peak is shorter; is the Rook card still where you die?"
- **Work is pulled in by the run.** Wave cards, scaling, the build's collapse and a Warden's pass are done when this run needs them, not all at once. The first run's biomes, foes and Warden get finished first.
- **Then the next run**: a new seed with a new frame, planets and Warden. It keeps everything run 1 taught us, and adds only what it needs.
- **Every so often, a random seed**, to check the polish carries over to runs we didn't tune.

### What it records
Every run writes one file:
- **The run**: seed, frame, chart, the route taken, every reward offered and picked, every dealer purchase, and the time spent in menus.
- **Each stage**:
  - the build at the start
  - time, and hull lost by source (which foe, which bullet, hazard, wall)
  - kills and time to kill by enemy kind
  - damage dealt per part and graft
  - verbs used (dash, perfects, parries, heavies, specials, welds, takedowns)
- **Each Warden**: time and hits taken per phase and per move, and when the welds came.
- **Your inputs**, frame by frame. The sim is deterministic, so I can replay any moment headless, see exactly what happened, and cut a clip of it (`tools/record.ts`).
- **A one-tap rating** at the end of a stage or Warden (fun / meh / unfair), and an optional line at the end of the run. Skippable.

### How it gets to me
- **Play on the dev server** (`just serve`, then `?run=<seed>` for the fixed run). Every run is recorded there, and with `?rec` anywhere else; `?norec` turns it off.
- **The dev server saves each run** to `playtests/` after every stage, stamped with the commit it was flown on (`server/playtests.ts`). Raw files stay out of git. Without a dev server, the file downloads at the run's end.
- **`just playtest-report`** sums up the latest day's runs (`--all`, or name the files): the route, each stage's hull and rating, deaths and what did it, hull lost by source, times to kill against the targets, Warden phase times and hits by move, damage by part, the picks, and which buttons nobody pressed.
- **`just replay <file> --stage 3`** flies a stage again headless, checks it ends where it did, and prints its timeline: every hit, by whom, and where. `--browser` shows it in the game, and `--video` records it (`tools/record.ts`).
- **The autopilot is checked against you.** Where its hull loss differs from yours, its bench verdict bands get recalibrated, so the bench predicts human play between playtests.

## What we keep from the cut

Nothing is deleted before the playtests say so. In the first slice, the cut systems are unhooked from the run, but the code stays. Once run 1 feels perfect, whatever wasn't missed is deleted. Things to keep in mind:

- **Boarding and the minigames** are self-contained and well made. If one is missed, it can come back as a rare reward node later. That's a decision for after the simple run is proven.
- **The NPCs and events' cast** stay alive as the dealers and Warden lines.
- **The lore** of the core, the factions and the Wardens stays. Only the systems built on top go.

## Open questions

- **The shield**: cut, as above, or keep it in place of the Weld?
- **Run length**: five stages a sector, or four?
- **Meta progression**: today there is none. A light version would add each Warden's parts to the pool once beaten (Dead Cells blueprints). Wait until the run itself is fun.
