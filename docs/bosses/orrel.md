# Weight-Captain Orrel (the Throat)

*The Fourteenth Weight's captain, holding the forced gate out of the Reach.* [The Qaushen Throne](../factions/qaushen-throne.md). Fortress. **Designed in outline, not built:** no fight in the game. How it's told: [tone](../lore/tone.md).

## Who he is

Tamas Orrel is an Ossevi, with thirty-one scars across his palms, one for each generation since his people forced their gates and tore forty lanes. He was born on the Throat, to Ballast, and commands the garrison that holds it: the Fourteenth Weight, conscripts sent through in 241 L who will never go home, and their children. His fort is the ***Plumbline***, a ring of counterweights hung round the Throat's mouth on cables a hundred kilometres long. Every ship that crosses leans the Throat a little, and the Weight weighs it and pays for it in people.

- **Caught between:** the Throne that counts him, and a lane his own people would have known better than to force.
- **Owes:** his soldiers a reason for being here. He hasn't got one, so he gives them discipline instead.
- **Wants from you:** to count you on the way out. No ship leaves the Reach uncounted, and you carry more crossed metal than anyone. He'd rather you stopped and were weighed; his orders say he can't let you not.
- **Sad truth:** the Throat is forced, and each ship he lets through tears it a little more. He keeps open the thing that killed his people, and he is the one person on it who knows exactly what that means.
- **The call:** the Engine levels lanes by carrying ballast back. If it wakes, the Weight could go home, through the one machine the Throne forbids. In his last phase his soldiers hear the tone through the Throat, and he lets them go.
- **Vibe:** a garrison chapel at the end of the world. Drums in step, a hymn from a world nobody here has seen, cables singing in the wind of the lean.

### His words

- **As he comes:** `WEIGHT-CAPTAIN ORREL: "STAND TO BE COUNTED, PILOT."`
- **Under the bar:** he counts you, in drill. `COUNTS: ONE, HANGING BACK`, `COUNTS: ONE, AT REST`, `COUNTS: ONE, ON THE LEFT`, `COUNTS: ONE, IN A HURRY`, `COUNTS: ONE, TOO CLOSE`, `COUNTS: ONE, NOT WHERE IT WAS`.
- **As he goes:** `"COUNTED. GO."`

## The dance (rough)

| Move | What it does | The answer |
|---|---|---|
| **COUNTERWEIGHT** | a weight on its cable swings down across a lane, drawn from the fort to the end of its arc | off the arc; the cable cuts too |
| **THE LEAN** | the Throat leans: a slow drift across the whole field, bending your shots and his, and you with them | fly against it, and aim off |
| **FORMATION** | crewed escorts in line round a Zhaun **count-ship**; when the count-ship falls, the escorts break off and go | kill the count-ship, not the soldiers |
| **THE COUNT** | the count-ship's tally beams sweep the field in fives | between the marks |

**Phase I: STAND TO.** By the drill: every move in order, escorts in step.
**Phase II: THE TEAR.** You've fought on a forced gate long enough to lean it: the drift never stops, and comes harder each time.
**Phase III: HOME.** The Engine's tone comes through the Throat. The escorts break off, not beaten: called home. Orrel fights on alone, slower, the counterweights let down one by one, and doesn't call anyone back.

**How it ends:** he lowers the last weight, and lets you through.

## Where it sits

- **Only once the Engine is awake.** On a run whose Glacis Warden is beaten (Glacis is always the third sector's Warden when it's in the run), the way out is the Throat, and Orrel is a fourth gate. On other runs the Throat stays a rumour. That keeps him rare, and his last phase always has a reason: the Engine has just woken, so its tone really does come through the Throat.
- **A choice first, then a fight.** At the Throat he hails you, and you choose:
  - **Be weighed.** You pay the Throat's toll: a share of the run's scrap, weighed against what you carry. He lets you through, the run ends, and the Throne knows your name.
  - **Run the Throat.** The fight. Win and you keep everything.
  - It's the lean, charged at the one gate that can't afford it, and he says so: `"IT ISN'T A FINE. IT'S WHAT THE LANE COSTS."`
- **Family: lattice**, in Throne colours. The Throne buys cores and has spent fourteen centuries trying to build its own Engine, so its ships are core-built like everyone's, but crewed: no new tell grammar, the draw as always. They still listen to the Engine now and then: their cores are reset like Vantorr's.
